## Yiling first, because she is the most useful data point

She said no to *"business solutions for her clients"*. That is what she heard, and hearing it is a
reasonable response: a broker does not want to be a software reseller, and being asked to push a
vendor's product at the people who trust her is a cost to her relationships, not a benefit.

**She was not rejecting the product. She was rejecting a channel role nobody wants.**

So do not email her, and do not treat the note as "wrong broker". Emailing someone who declined is
how you get talked about in a six-person network, and the note says the pitch missed — which is
worth more than her yes would have been.

**What changes because of her:** none of the emails below ask a broker to give anything to a client,
resell anything, or introduce a product. The ask is about *their* listings and *their* fee.

---

## The frame all four use

Not "software for your clients". Two things a broker already has an opinion about:

1. **The conversation they hate.** Telling an owner his business will not fetch what he thinks
   because it all runs through him. Every one of them has that conversation and none of them enjoys
   it.
2. **Their fee is a percentage.** A business that presents as transferable sells higher, and sells
   at all — and a listing that does not sell is months of their work for nothing.

That is the whole argument and it needs no product explanation. **Do not quote a monthly price.**
The fee is the owner's problem, not the broker's, and naming it invites the reseller reading that
lost Yiling.

⚠️ **Do not put a valuation number in any of these.** The multiples are US data wound back
(register A1 — the ceiling can exceed its own cited source, and it overclaims hardest on the larger
businesses a broker cares about). These are the most numerate readers this product will ever have.
Get the Australian bands from them first; do not show them arithmetic you already know is soft.

## Andrew Hahn — he is calling, so send nothing

He answered a voicemail with an SMS saying he will ring. That is a better outcome than any email
produces, and emailing into it costs more than it gains: it either pre-empts the conversation or
gives him something to read instead of a reason to call. **Wait.**

**Him specifically.** Northern Territory — a small market of owner-operated trades and service
businesses, often remote, frequently one person with everything in his head and no succession
anywhere near. If owner-dependency is the constraint anywhere in the country it is there, which
makes him a good early read and a bad person to be wrong in front of.

**Open with the moment, not the product.** *"How often does a business come to you that would sell
fine except everything runs through the owner?"* If he says "constantly", he has made the argument
himself and nothing needs pitching. If he says "that's not really what kills them up here", that is
worth more than a yes, and the honest answer is to ask what does.

**Ask, then stop talking:**
- What actually costs his listings — is it owner-dependency, or price expectations, or finance, or
  no buyers in the territory at all?
- His niche bands. Trades, hospitality, transport, services — what does each fetch as a multiple of
  profit? Simon Crisp offered his unprompted and it is the fix for the model's weakest part.
- Whether he meets owners early enough to do anything about it, or only once they have decided.

**Do not:** quote a monthly price, put a valuation figure in front of him, or ask him to give
anything to a client — that last one is what lost Yiling. If he wants to look, he gets an account on
his own brokerage, never a live listing.

**Then** email — and it is version A, the warm one, not B.
