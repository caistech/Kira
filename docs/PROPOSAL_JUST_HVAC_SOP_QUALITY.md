# Proposal — Just HVAC: standard operating procedures that actually get followed

Peter McDowell · All Skills Group Pty Ltd
Prepared 11 August 2026 · for discussion Friday

## What I heard

You want SOPs and an ISO 9001 system for Just HVAC, with Claude sitting across both as the
gatekeeper — nothing leaves the place unless it has been through the standard. You have taken five
companies through 9001, so you have the material; what you do not have is the time to turn it into
this business's version, and the honest reason you gave was the right one: *writing procedures is a
nightmare, and the first procedure you have to write is the procedure for writing procedures.*

You also said the thing that shapes everything below:

> *"The value of your company is a multiple of the quality of your standard operating procedures."*

And then the complaint underneath it — that quality systems stopped being about quality and became
about documentation, and the people administering them do not understand what they are for.

So this proposal is built around that, not around producing a shelf of documents.

## What I am not proposing

**I am not proposing to write your procedures.** You can do that yourself, with Claude, and you told
me exactly how. Charging you for it would be selling you something you already own.

The work is in the two things that come after the writing, which is where every quality system I have
ever seen fell over — including mine. We had the manuals. Big shelf, like the one behind me. We
ticked the box every year and nobody used them until something went wrong.

## What I am proposing

**1. The standard.** Your existing 9001 documentation turned into Just HVAC's, structured so a
machine can check against it rather than just a person reading it. Including the procedure for
writing procedures, so the next twenty are consistent without you in the room.

**2. The gate.** Nothing goes out that has not been through the standard. A quote, a report, an email
from one of the team whose English is their second language — it gets brought up to the standard
before it leaves, not corrected after it lands with a client. Your McDonald's point: one way to make
the hamburger, regardless of who is on shift.

**3. The nudge.** The part that makes procedures real. Every step that has a date gets one — has this
been done, is it due, who owns it — so that following the procedure is the path of least resistance
rather than an act of discipline. You do not think about the thirty-seven people who need a nudge;
the system does.

**4. And the part I think you will care about most.**

You said quality became about documentation instead of about quality. The way out of that is to stop
producing documentation as a separate activity and let it fall out of the work being done. If the
nudge captures the checkbox and the photo at the time, then **the audit trail builds itself** — and
the 9001 evidence pack is a by-product of running the business properly rather than a fortnight of
someone's life before an audit.

That is the difference between a system you maintain and a system that maintains itself.

## Designed around Noel, not around me

Noel owns this from the day he starts, and you were straight about where he sits with technology.
So the test I am designing to is: **Noel never has to learn a piece of software.** He asks for the
procedure and gets it. He is told what is outstanding rather than going to look. Where he has to
respond, he responds the way he already communicates.

If the system needs him to become technical, it has failed, and I would rather say that up front than
discover it in week three.

## Where your data goes

You raised this and it deserves a straight answer rather than a reassurance.

The procedures and the quality manual stay in your systems. Nothing is stored by me.

The gate is the part that needs a decision, because bringing a document up to standard means the
content passes through a model. Two options, and we should pick deliberately:

- **Commercial model, with a written boundary** on what never passes through it — client commercial
  terms, pricing, personal information. Fastest to stand up, and honest about its limits.
- **Open-weight model on your own hardware.** Nothing leaves the building. More setup, more cost,
  and the right answer if the material is sensitive enough to warrant it.

My recommendation is to start with the first and a clearly drawn line, and keep the second as a path
rather than a promise. Either way it is your decision, not a default.

## Four weeks

| | |
|---|---|
| **Week 1** | The standard. Your 9001 material becomes Just HVAC's, plus the SOP set for service and maintenance. |
| **Week 2** | The gate. Outgoing documents and email brought to standard before they leave. |
| **Week 3** | The nudge. Dates, ownership, site checkpoints, and a weekly list of what has not happened. |
| **Week 4** | Noel. Onboarded by using it rather than being trained on it — and the first audit trail checked against what 9001 will actually ask for. |

**Service and maintenance only.** Small contracts follows once this one is standing. Doing both at
once is how these things become a project instead of a system.

## What "worked" means

Falsifiable, so Friday-in-four-weeks is a fact rather than an opinion:

1. **Noel finds and follows the right procedure without asking you.**
2. **An email from a team member goes to a client at the standard, without you reading it first.**
3. **A month of completed work has an audit trail nobody sat down to write.**
4. **Your own time on quality is under two hours a week.**

If two of those four are not true, I have not finished, and I would rather keep working than invoice.
