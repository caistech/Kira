## First — the classification question, answered honestly

The toolkit sorts AI into **Public**, **Embedded**, and **Enterprise**. Kira does not sit cleanly in
any of them, for a reason worth stating plainly: **those three categories describe tools *your firm*
uses in client work. Kira is not one.** Your client uses it directly, on their own account, for
their own business. It is not in your practice stack and produces nothing that enters your file
unless your client chooses to show you.

If you want to classify it anyway, the honest placement is **between Embedded and Enterprise**:

| Enterprise AI criterion | Kira |
|---|---|
| Closed-loop, per-account isolation | **Yes** — each account has its own assistant; one account's memory is never visible to another |
| Inputs not used to train models | **Yes** — we do not use what a client tells Kira to train anyone's models |
| No external data sharing | **No.** Conversations are processed by ElevenLabs, and responses are generated by third-party AI model providers. Data does leave our systems. |

We are not going to call that "Enterprise AI" when the third line is a no.

## The single most useful thing to know

**The valuation number is arithmetic, not AI.**

The figure your client sees — their current value, their potential, the gap — is produced by a
deterministic calculation in code from the figures they enter: sector multiples calibrated on
BizBuySell 2025 closed-deal data, a weighted readiness score, a floor and a ceiling. No language
model is involved in producing it. Given the same inputs it returns the same number, every time,
and it can be recomputed and audited years later because every valuation stores its inputs and the
version of the model that read them.

The AI is the **conversation** — the assistant your client talks to. Those are two different systems
and it matters which one you are being asked to trust.

## The ten questions, as they apply to us

Answers to the toolkit's self-assessment where it points at a vendor rather than at your firm.

**"Have you reviewed your AI vendors' data processing terms?" (Q4)**
Our subprocessors, and what each does: **Supabase** (database), **Vercel** (hosting), **Resend**
(email), **Stripe** (payments — card numbers never reach our systems), **ElevenLabs** (the voice
conversation), and **third-party AI model providers** that generate the assistant's responses.
**Some process or store data outside Australia, including in the United States and the European
Union.** We do not offer Australian data residency. That is stated in our privacy policy rather than
buried in it.

**"Do clients consent to AI processing their data?" (Q6)**
Yes, at two points. ElevenLabs presents its own consent notice before any voice conversation
begins — it is their notice and it is accurate: they are a third party processing what is said.
Separately, our terms and privacy policy are accepted at signup, and **the version accepted is
recorded**, so it is answerable which wording a given client agreed to.

**"Is there a documented review step before AI output reaches a client?" (Q5)**
Not in the sense the question intends, and we would rather say so. Kira talks to your client
directly; there is no human in that loop. What we do instead: the valuation figures are not
AI-generated (above), and the assistant is positioned as an assistant, not as advice. **Nothing Kira
produces is presented to your client as accounting, tax or financial advice**, and nothing is filed
or lodged anywhere.

**"Do you have a response protocol for when an AI output turns out to be wrong?" (Q9)**
Partly. Every screen carries an in-product reporting widget that files the issue directly to our
engineering backlog, and a person reads it. **We do not yet have a written, published AI incident
response standard** with defined notification timeframes. It is on the list; it is not done.

**"Does your PI insurer know you use AI?" (Q8)**
That question is about your firm, not us. Worth noting that a referral is not you using AI in client
work — but your insurer's view of referrals is between you and them, and we will not tell you it is
fine.

## What your client's data does *not* do

- **It is not used to train models.** Not ours, not our providers'.
- **It is not sold**, and the valuation figures — turnover, profit, owner dependence — are **not
  used to build a market database**. For most owners that is the most sensitive information they
  will type into anything.
- **It is not visible to you.** See below.

## What you can see as an introducer — and why you can trust the limit

You can see that your client signed up, roughly where they are, and whether their valuation is
moving. You cannot see their conversations, what the assistant remembers, or anything they have told
it.

**That limit is enforced in the database, not in the interface.** The only sanctioned read path from
an introducer to their clients is a single SQL function that returns status and movement columns and
has no access to content. There is no screen, no export and no mistake that could widen it, because
the widening would have to be a schema change.

We mention this because "we promise not to look" is worth very little, and an accountant introducing
their own client is entitled to a better answer than a promise.

## Retention, deletion and rights

- Account and business information for as long as the account exists, and briefly after, to settle
  anything outstanding.
- What the assistant remembers, until the client deletes it — **which they can do without closing
  their account.** Everything else keeps working; it simply starts again.
- Billing records for as long as tax law requires, typically seven years.
- Clients can request a copy, a correction, or deletion, free of charge, and can complain to the
  **OAIC** if we mishandle it.

## One thing worth telling your clients about the regulation

The TPB's guidance on AI use by tax practitioners — issued as an exposure draft (**TPB(I)
D62/2026**) in March 2026 — was **finalised as TPB(GS) 55/2026 on 22 July 2026**. Some AI-governance
material still in circulation, including toolkits produced before that date, describes it as a draft
and advises checking the current status. It is now settled.

It governs **your** use of AI in delivering tax agent services. Our reading is that referring a
client to a product they use themselves does not engage it — but that is our reading, not advice,
and your own compliance adviser's view is the one that counts.
