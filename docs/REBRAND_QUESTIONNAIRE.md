# Rebrand Questionnaire — Kira

Adapted for Kira from Gareth Newman's Mnemo questionnaire, August 2026. Structure, numbering and
instructions are his. Eight questions have been swapped because the originals assume a
developer-tool sold to developers, and Kira is neither.

**Questions changed from Gareth's original: 8, 9, 13, 23, 24, 30, 32, 38, 41.** Everything else is
his wording, unaltered. Where a question was swapped it is marked *(adapted)* so Gareth can see
what was changed and push back if the swap was wrong.

---

## Before you start

### Why this is being asked

Gareth's case for moving Mnemo was discoverability, not taste: five other live products in the AI
memory space were using the same name, three on better domains, and someone who heard about Mnemo
at a conference and searched later found four other people first.

**The equivalent sweep has not been run for Kira.** It should be, before you spend an hour on this.
The one collision already known: **Kira Systems**, a contract-analysis AI company acquired by
Litera — squarely in professional-services AI, and known to precisely the accountants, lawyers and
brokers your handover document is built for. Whether that is fatal, survivable or already lapsed is
a question for Gareth's research rather than a judgement to make here. There may be others.

So treat this as filling in the raw material *while* the collision question is being answered — not
as a decision already taken to rename.

### How this works

You fill this in. Gareth takes the answers and comes back with a shortlist of names that should
feel like they came out of you rather than out of a generator. Once a name is locked, the rest of
the identity gets built to match it: logo, colours, type, the tone of the copy, the domain. Whatever
still fits the new name is kept and whatever does not is dropped.

### Time and effort

Roughly 45 to 60 minutes for the whole thing. The questions marked **(key)** are the ones that do
most of the work. If you only have twenty minutes, do those and leave the rest.

Please do not polish your answers. Half-finished thoughts, tangents, and the things you would be a
bit embarrassed to say out loud are usually where the good names hide. Write like you are talking to
him, not like you are filing something.

If you get stuck on a question, skip it and say you skipped it. That is information too.

**Q8. (key) *(adapted)*** Kira currently leads with several different claims: the handover document
an owner can hand to an advisor, the transferability score and the value gap, the fact that she
listens and captures rather than making him type, that she leaves his own position out of anything

## Part 5. Language, roots and raw material

*This is the section that actually produces names, so it is worth the most of your time.*

**Q21. (key)** Words, phrases or ideas you are drawn to. From anywhere at all. Your field, someone
else's field, books, physics, law, geology, music, cooking, sailing, building. They do not have to
relate to the product. Just list them as they come.

Answer:

**Q22. (key)** Any languages, places or personal history you would like drawn on? Family languages,
where you grew up, something out of your own background. Names that come from here tend to be the
ones founders never get tired of, and they are far less likely to collide with someone else in the
category.

Answer:

**Q23. *(adapted)*** Words you are sick of seeing in this category. Be brutal. To get you started:
every human first name (Ada, Ava, Aria, Iris, Mia, Nora), copilot, assistant, advisor, mentor,
coach, buddy, sidekick, sage, oracle, genie, nexus, legacy, succession, exit, anything ending in
-ly, anything ending in -ai, and every last .ai domain.

Answer:

**Q24. (key) *(adapted)*** Below are metaphors that could fit what you have built. Tell me which ones
land, which ones feel wrong, and which ones you had not considered.

- **The right hand.** The one who knows how it is done because they have watched it done.
- **The successor.** What is left behind so the place still runs.
- **The handover.** The set of keys, and the walk round the building that goes with them.
- **Institutional memory.** What a business knows that is not written anywhere.
- **The foreman's daybook.** How the job actually runs, in the words of the man running it.
- **The understudy.** Knows every line, has never been on stage.
- **The deed box.** What you would want found if you were not there to be asked.
- **The survey.** Measuring what is there before anyone puts a price on it.
- **The second set of eyes.** Sees the thing the owner has stopped seeing.

Answer:

**Q25. (key)** Is there a word you have already been circling privately? Everyone has one. Even if
you think it is a bad idea, write it down and tell me why you keep coming back to it.

Answer:

**Q26.** Is there a story behind why you built this? Something that happened, something that

## Part 6. Shape and sound

**Q27.** Syllables. One, two, three, or no strong preference?

Answer:

**Q28.** Rank these five styles from most to least appealing.

- A real English word used in a new context. Stripe, Slack, Anchor.
- An altered real word. Lyft, Flickr, Tumblr.
- A compound of two real words. Snowflake, Firebase, Datadog.
- An invented word that simply sounds right. Vercel, Twilio, Kubo.
- A word borrowed from another language, or a dead one. Vestigo, Kanso, Datum.

Answer:

**Q29.** Does the name have to work when said out loud, on a call, to someone who has never seen it
written down? Or is it mostly typed and read?

Answer:

**Q30. *(adapted)*** Your buyer is often in his sixties and may not have English as a first language;
so may his accountant. Does the name have to be pronounceable on sight by someone in that position,
and spellable back to you after hearing it once? My own view is that for something sold by word of
mouth through advisors the answer is yes, and it rules a lot of options out. Tell me if you
disagree.

Answer:

**Q31.** Sounds you like or cannot stand. Hard consonants like K, T and X. Soft ones like S, F and
TH. Vowel endings. Doubled letters. Anything you know irritates you.

Answer:

**Q32. (key) *(adapted)*** The spell-it-over-the-phone test. An accountant is going to say this name
to a client, and that client is going to type it into a search bar an hour later from memory. Does
the name have to survive that intact — no ambiguous spelling, no silent letters, no "is that with a
K or a C"? Tell me whether that is a hard requirement, and what the maximum length is before it gets
annoying to say.

Answer:

**Q33.** Does it need to work as a verb, as in "just X it"? Some names benefit from that. Most do
