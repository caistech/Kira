# Ray's walkthrough of Kira

Hi Dennis,

**Persona:** Ray, 66. Plumbing business, 31 years, 9 staff. Turnover about $2.4M, I keep roughly $260k after I pay myself, and the vans and gear are worth maybe $220k. Thinking about selling. Haven't told the staff, haven't told my kids, and some weeks I haven't told my wife.
**URL:** https://kira-rho.vercel.app
**Goal:** work out whether this thing actually understands my situation, and whether I'd tell it anything true about my business.
**Duration:** about 45 minutes, desktop then phone.
**Date:** 28 July 2026

I'll say up front: parts of this are the best-aimed writing I've read from a software company. And parts of it would have had me out the door in ninety seconds. Both are worth knowing.

---

## The landing page

- **The first paragraph is aimed straight at me and it lands.** "This is for one person: the owner in their sixties, three or four decades in, with a profitable business that runs on them." I've had four people try to sell me something this year and none of them started by describing me correctly. Then: *"Most owners start this before they've told anyone."* That sentence is the reason I kept reading instead of closing the tab. Somebody there understands that the secrecy is the actual condition of my life right now, not a footnote.
- **Then I hit the numbers box and my guard went straight back up.** WALK AWAY $150k / TODAY $600k / CAPTURED $2.5M, and underneath, "The $1.9M gap is the knowledge in your head." That is a four-fold jump. I have a broker who's just told me I'm worth less than I think, and I half believe him. A stranger's website telling me I'm worth 4× is the oldest noise in the world. There's no "example" label on it either, so I read it as a claim. (For what it's worth: when I actually ran my own numbers, the gap came out at 1.75×, not 4×. So the shop window is overselling the shop by more than double. See below.)
- **The picture at the top.** A young woman in a headset, computer-made, in a pink and purple ring. I'm being asked to talk about the thing I've spent 35 years building, to a cartoon receptionist. It doesn't say "serious" to me, it says "app".
- **"Your fractional exec."** That's the browser tab, that's the yellow box, that's the whole framing. I do not know what a fractional exec is. Neither does anyone I drink with. Two paragraphs later you say "the chief of staff you could never justify hiring", which I understand instantly. Lead with that.
- **"Part of the Corporate AI Solutions Voice AI Suite."** That's the first thing under the header and it means nothing to me. It's the kind of line that tells me I'm buying software, not help.
- **The four quotes with no names on them.** Every one is labelled "Business". No name, no town, no trade. I understand you may not have anyone willing to be named — you've just told me nobody in my position talks about this. But an unattributed quote reads as invented, and one real named plumber would be worth more than all four.
- **I clicked "Pricing" in the menu and there is no price on it.** It jumps to a section headed "See the number. Then decide" which says the fee is "a small fraction of what you stand to unlock" and "that's why there's no price list here". I read the whole page twice. There is no number anywhere. Calling a menu item "Pricing" and putting no price behind it is the thing every dodgy operator does and I have a working nose for it. Call it "How you'd pay" or put a range on it.
- Small one, but I noticed: the icon on that pricing block is a **British pound note**. On an Australian product priced in Australian dollars.

**Opportunity:** Label the $150k/$600k/$2.5M box as an example, and make the example a realistic multiple (about 1.7–1.8×, which is what your own calculator actually produces) rather than 4×. The honesty of the rest of the site is your best asset and that one box is spending it. And either put a number behind the word "Pricing" or stop using the word.

## The free valuation — the eleven questions

- **The intro page is good.** "Answer 11 short questions and see three honest numbers." "About 3 minutes. Nothing to sign up for." "Your answers are kept on this device as you go, so you can stop and come back." That last line matters more than you probably think — it tells me nothing's leaving this laptop yet.
- **Then question one asked what industry I'm in and I typed "plumber". It said: "No sector match for 'plumber'."** I sat and looked at that for a good few seconds. A thing that is about to tell me what my life's work is worth doesn't know what a plumber is.
- **It gets worse when you poke it.** I tried again and typed "plumbing" — the full, correct, actual name of the trade. **Also nothing.** No suggestion, no warning, no confirmation, just a blank. It was only when I chopped the word short to "plumb" that up came *Plumbing — Construction*. So: type four or five letters and it works; type the whole word correctly and it silently fails. That's backwards. The people most likely to type "plumbing" into a box marked industry are plumbers.
- And the failure isn't even honest — with "plumber" it at least *told me* it hadn't matched. With "plumbing" it showed me nothing at all, no match and no warning, so I had no idea whether it had taken it or not. If I'd pressed Next there I'd never have known which multiple it used on me.
- **Question 2, turnover.** I typed 2400000 and the box showed me `2400000`. No commas. The hint above the box shows "e.g. 2,000,000" *with* commas, so it knows how to do it. I had to count digits on the screen with my finger to be sure I hadn't typed twenty-four million. This is the single most important number in the whole exercise.
- **Question 3 is the best-written question on the site.** "What's left after all costs, plus the salary and perks you pay yourself (often called SDE)." Then it works an example: *"If the business kept $150k after costs and paid you $50k in salary and perks, add them together and enter $200,000."* That's how you explain something to me. No jargon left dangling.
  - One catch: I know one number — what I keep after paying myself. To answer that question properly I'd have to go and find what I pay myself, which means opening the accounts, which means my bookkeeper might ask why. On a "3 minute, nothing to sign up for" promise, that's the question that sends me away to get a folder.
- Questions 4 to 10 were quick and plain and I could answer every one honestly without thinking. "If you took a 3-month holiday tomorrow, what happens?" — *It would struggle, I'm heavily involved day to day.* That's a true answer and it stung a bit, which is probably the point.
- Question 7, the one about revenue concentration — "especially ones who deal with you personally" — is aimed exactly at my three builders. I answered it truthfully because it was a tick box with no name attached. I would not have typed their names in.
- 11 questions, no email asked for, no card, no sign-up. **You kept that promise exactly.** That is worth a lot.

**Opportunity:** Fix the industry box so the full word works — "plumber", "plumbing", "sparky", "electrician", "chippy". Add trade nicknames as aliases; that list is 30 lines of work and it's the very first thing every tradesman sees. Put comma separators in the money fields. And on the profit question, let me enter the two numbers separately ("what the business kept" and "what you pay yourself") and add them for me — I know both of those better than I know the total.

## The result

This is where you got me back.

- **Walk away $220,000. Worth today $582,459 (~2.2× SDE). With your knowledge captured $1,019,169 (~3.9× SDE). The value locked in your head right now: $436,710. Transferability score 32/100.**
- $582k. My broker's been telling me it's less than I think. This number is in the neighbourhood he's in. **It didn't flatter me, and that's the reason I believed it.** If it had said $1.6M I'd have shut the laptop.
- **"Why the number is what it is" is the best thing on the site.** The car-bought-sight-unseen analogy is exactly right and it's how a buyer thinks. It doesn't tell me I'm being ripped off; it explains why the discount is rational. I read it twice.
- **"Where that value is hiding" adds up.** Owner dependence $152,394, systems $151,635, recurring revenue $75,818, client diversification $56,863. That's $436,710, which is the gap number exactly. I checked it on a bit of paper because that's what I do. **It reconciled.** That did more for my trust than any of the paragraphs.
- **Now the things that took it back off you.**
  - **"Walk away $220,000" with a caption saying "a quick auction typically returns 40–60c in the dollar."** So which is it? $220,000, or $88,000 to $132,000? You've printed one number in big type and contradicted it in small type directly underneath. I've been quoting jobs for 35 years — that's the sort of thing that makes me wonder what else is loose.
  - **$582,459.** To the dollar. On something you've labelled an indicative estimate. Nobody knows my business to the dollar; you've made a guess look like a survey. Round it, or better, give me a band — "$550k to $620k". A range reads honest. A precise figure on a guess reads like it's trying to sound clever.
  - **And then the small print at the bottom.** "Sector medians are BizBuySell's 2025 **US** small-business sale data... There is no equivalent Australian dataset... We use it as an indicative benchmark, not an Australian market quote — the absolute number should be checked against local evidence before anyone acts on it."
    I want to be fair here: that is a genuinely honest disclosure and most people wouldn't write it. **But it's at the very bottom, after you've shown me a number in enormous type.** What it actually means is that the number I came here for is not an Australian number. That belongs next to the figure, not 2,000 words below it. Say it out loud: *"benchmarked on US sale data because Australia doesn't publish this — treat the shape as right and the dollar as indicative."* Told early, that builds trust. Found late, it feels like it was hidden.
  - The result also vanished. I came back to the site later and the whole thing was gone — no result, no numbers, and the pricing page just said "Let's find your number first". You did say it's kept on this device, so that's on me, but there's no way to email it to myself either. If I do this on my phone at the yard I can't show my wife on the laptop that night without doing it all again.

**Opportunity:** Move the US-data disclosure up next to the number and put it in your own plain voice. Show a range not a to-the-dollar figure. Make "Walk away" agree with its own caption. And offer to email me the result — I know that's an email address you'd like to have, and honestly, at that moment, having just seen a number I believed, it's the one moment I'd hand it over.

## What it costs

- Clicking through from the result takes you to a page that finally shows the price: **$999/month + GST**, framed as "about 2.7% a year of what you stand to unlock". The GST is stated. Good — I'd have asked.
- **$999 a month.** Twelve grand a year plus GST, out of the $260k I keep. That's a real number and I need a moment with it. It's not outrageous for what's described. But I've now been told twice that "her fee is set to the size of that gap", and "why is the price different for different businesses". It isn't. It's $999. If it's one price, say so — one price is easier to trust than a personalised one you won't show me until the end.
- **The page contradicts itself on the free bit, twice on the same card.** The headline says "your first 30 days are free". The line under the price says "**Free while we are in beta.** $999 + GST/month once billing goes live." Those are two different offers. Which one am I agreeing to?
- **"Beta · payments not live — Stripe is in test mode."** Then again in the fine print: "no card is charged, no real subscription is created, and nothing will come out in 30 days." I respect that you said it. I really do. But read it as me: I'm being asked to put the crown jewels of a 31-year business into something that isn't finished enough to take money yet. That's the sentence that would make me wait six months and see if you're still there.
- **And then the sentence that actually stopped me cold.** Under "What happens when you talk to Kira": *"You only ever talk to Kira. **Behind her is a quiet team that does the actual work** and remembers everything — so you never have to."* The icon next to the step below it is two people.
  Who is the quiet team? Are they people? Do people read what I said? Because four paragraphs later, step 5 says "only you, and anyone you choose, can ever see it." Those two statements cannot both be true, and I'm the wrong customer to leave that gap in front of. I have not told my wife I'm selling. "A quiet team" is precisely the phrase that makes me not tell you either.

**Opportunity:** Kill "a quiet team" or explain it in the same breath. If it means software agents, say software. If it means a person ever reads a transcript, say that too and say when — I can live with an honest yes, I can't live with the ambiguity. And pick one story on the free month: 30 days, or free-while-beta. Right now the most expensive page on the site is the least clear one.

## The Business Genome

This is the page that would sell it to me, and I nearly never saw it.

- **The example is a plumbing business, 31 years old, 9 staff.** That is me on the nose. First time on the whole site I felt like it was written for my trade rather than at it.
- And the content is real. "Three builders supply roughly 60% of turnover — Hartley Constructions, Vaughan Homes and Ridge Developments. All three came through the owner personally; Hartley since 1998. None are on a written contract — work is allocated by a phone call to the owner, usually on a Friday." **That is my business.** Three builders, one since the nineties, no contracts, a Friday phone call. I read that and thought: they've actually sat with someone like me.
- **The bit that earns trust is the "Still only in your head" box.** It names what it *hasn't* got: *"Why the fourth builder stopped calling in 2023 — and whether that relationship is recoverable."* A tool that tells me what it doesn't know is a tool I'd tell things to. Same with "Captured — not yet confirmed" against a fact, and "You confirmed this · Conversation, 12 March". That's a system showing its working. I've hired people who wouldn't do that.
- **"A copy of the data... If you stop paying us, you keep it."** and "A handover document one pack a buyer's accountant or solicitor can read cold." That's the first time on the whole site I could see what $999 buys. Not "a fractional exec" — a document my solicitor can read.
- **The privacy-mode section is the single most persuasive thing you've written.** "Privacy mode — on the roadmap, and not built yet... We are telling you it is not finished because most owners reading this have not told their staff or their family yet, and you should know exactly what we can and cannot do before you say a word to us. Today Kira listens only when you open a conversation and press the button." You've told me the bad news before I asked, in the exact terms I was worried about. **That is the paragraph that would get the truth out of me.**

**And now the problem.**

- **/genome is not in the menu.** How it works, Pricing, Advisors, About — no Genome. The only way in is a small underlined phrase buried in the fourth line of the hero paragraph.
- **The valuation result does not link to it.** The $999 page does not link to it. I checked every link on that page: Redo my valuation, Find my gap, Report a problem, corporateaisolutions.com. **That's it.** So the page where you ask me for twelve grand a year has no route to the only page that shows me what I'd get.
- If I hadn't been poking around out of stubbornness I'd have made the buy/don't-buy decision without ever seeing it.

**Opportunity:** Put "See an example Genome" in the main menu and, more importantly, as a button on the valuation result and directly under the $999. It's your best page by a distance and it's hidden. And once it's visible, make the plumbing example the *default* — the moment where a bloke like me goes "hang on, that's my business" is the whole sale.

## Signing in, and what's behind it

- The login page is fine. Email, password, a little eye to show the password, Forgot password, and "Email me a magic link" — I like that last one, I'm forever resetting passwords.
- **Then I signed in and landed on a black screen.** The whole site up to that point is warm cream and yellow. The login is white. Now it's black, with the cartoon woman again, saying "Meet Kira — **your friendly guide**". Not "your fractional exec". Different name for the same thing, third look in three screens.
- **It said "Choose your path" and there was one path.**
- **And there was no menu. No Settings. No Sign out.** The only link on the page was "Back to home", which puts me back on the sales page. I was now signed in, on a laptop, with my email address in an account, **and no visible way to sign out.** I don't like that at all. If a bookkeeper sat down at that laptop the next morning, what would she see?
- **The proper app exists — I just wasn't sent to it.** By guessing at the address I found `/dashboard`, and there it all is: a sidebar down the left with My Kiras, Knowledge, New Kira, and Settings and Sign out anchored at the bottom with my email under them. That's a real, tidy application. **Signing in doesn't take you there.** It takes you to the black cul-de-sac.
- **Settings is genuinely good** once you find it. Profile, "Your first month is free. There's no card on your account yet, so nothing can be charged", a usage bar showing $0.00 of $20 used and 30 days left, password, notifications, and delete account. Plain and honest. (Though note: the sales page told me you'd need my card, and Settings says there's no card and I won't be asked until a paid month starts. Third version of the payment story.)
- **Now the thing that matters most.** I did the valuation, I signed in, and **not one number from it is in there.** No $582k, no $436,710 gap, no transferability score, no Business Genome, no handover document, no sections at 54% and 41% documented. "Knowledge" turned out to be a place to upload files, not the Genome. So: everything you sold me on the way in — the Genome, the score, the pack my solicitor can read — **is on the marketing pages and not in the product.**
- What is in the product is "You don't have a Kira yet — Create your Kira", and "My Kiras", and "New Kira". I came here to sell my business. I now apparently have to make a Kira, and I might have several. I don't know what one is. On the front page Kira was a person; in here she's a thing you create and can have more than one of.

**Opportunity:** Send people to `/dashboard` when they sign in, or put the sidebar on `/start`. Right now the front door opens into a cupboard. Then carry my valuation into the account — the gap number, the four line items, the 32/100 — and let the Genome fill up against it. A progress bar that starts at 32 and creeps up is the only thing that would keep me paying $999 a month, and it's the one thing that isn't there.

## Would I tell it the truth?

You asked me to pay attention to this, so here it is straight.

**The two things nobody knows** are (a) the discount I've given Hartley for twenty-odd years that isn't written anywhere and that my son would never think to keep, and (b) why I won't touch strata work — which is a falling-out with a body corporate manager in 2014 that I'm not proud of. Both of those are worth real money to a buyer and both of them die with me.

**What made me more willing:**
- The Genome page naming its own gaps. "Still only in your head — why the fourth builder stopped calling in 2023." That's the same shape as my Hartley problem, written by someone who clearly knows owners have these. It told me the awkward stuff is *expected*, not shameful.
- The privacy paragraph admitting privacy mode isn't built, and *why* it's telling me before I say a word.
- "Your answers are kept on this device."
- The valuation not flattering me.
- Being able to answer the concentration question with a tick box instead of typing a name.

**What made me less willing:**
- **"Behind her is a quiet team that does the actual work."** That's the big one. Everything else on the site says it stays with me; that says there are people. I'd have stopped at the door.
- **Beta, Stripe in test mode, payments not live.** I'm not putting a 31-year secret into an unfinished thing.
- The Advisors page — which is in the main menu, so I read it — says a broker who introduces me can see that I signed up and how my valuation is moving, and gets 10% of what I pay, monthly, forever. I actually think publishing that is right and I'd rather know. But that's the *broker's* page. On **my** side of the site it's one line buried in a fold-out FAQ at the bottom. If my broker's link is what brought me here, I ought to be told on the first screen I see, not in an accordion.
- Nothing anywhere says who else at your end can read it, or what happens if you get bought, or if someone subpoenas you mid-sale.

**Honest answer:** No, not on day one. I'd tell it the boring true things — turnover, staff, the three builders exist. I would not name Hartley, I would not say a word about the discount, and I would not tell it about 2014. Not until "privacy mode" is built, "a quiet team" is explained, and the beta banner is gone.

**And would I sign up?** Not today. Close, though — closer than anything else that's been put in front of me. What stops me is not the $999. It's that when I got through the door, the thing you'd been describing for ten minutes wasn't in there.

## Other strategic feature suggestions

1. **A "who can see this" page, one click from everywhere.** One screen: who at your end can read it (and when), whether an introducing broker is attached to my account and exactly what they see, what happens if I stop paying, what happens if you're bought. For your customer that page is worth more than any feature. Put a link to it in the footer of every page and next to every input box that asks for something real.
2. **A "nobody knows I'm doing this" mode.** No emails to my address unless I ask. Nothing in the browser title that gives it away. A panic button that hides the screen. You already understand this problem better than anyone — you wrote "most owners start this before they've told anyone" on your own front page — but nothing in the product acts on it.
3. **Email me my valuation, and let me come back to it.** It's the one moment I'd hand over an address, and right now the result exists only in the browser I was standing in front of.
4. **A one-page thing I can put in front of my broker.** Not to sell him anything — so I can say "this says 2.2× and you said 2. Where's the difference?" Give me the argument, and you've made yourself useful in a conversation I'm having anyway.
5. **Show the gap closing.** 32/100 today. Show me 34 next month because I answered three questions about pricing. That's the receipt for the $999. Without it I'm paying a subscription to a conversation.
6. **A trade dictionary for the industry box.** Plumber, sparky, chippy, brickie, roofer, tiler, landscaper, mechanic, panel beater, earthmoving. Half your intended customers describe themselves with a word your box doesn't know.
7. **Drop "fractional exec" from the top of the page.** "The chief of staff you could never justify hiring" is your own line, it's on the same page, and I understood it immediately. The other one I had to work out.
8. **Name one real customer.** One. A plumber, a town, a first name. It's worth more than the four anonymous quotes put together, and right now those quotes are actively costing you credibility with a suspicious reader.
