Platform Feedback — Kira Walkthrough
Persona: Ray, 66, exiting owner (35 years, electrical & mechanical contracting, ~$2.4M turnover, told almost nobody) | URL: https://kira-rho.vercel.app | Goal: explore as a first-time visitor, run the free valuation, decide whether I'd trust this with what's in my head | Duration: ~45 min, desktop 1440 and phone 375

Landing page
- The first paragraph describes me so precisely it made me sit back — "the owner in their sixties, three or four decades in, with a profitable business that runs on them", and then "Most owners start this before they've told anyone." I've had that sentence in my head for two years and never said it out loud. That's the best thing on the site. Whatever else I say below, don't touch that paragraph.
- Then it undoes some of it with the styling. The brand is green, the buttons are pink, the big numbers are purple, and there are emoji headings — 💰, 🛠️, 🤝, 🏦. I'm the man in the photo on your homepage and I don't use emoji in a quote. It reads as though the page was written for me and then decorated by someone thirty years younger. The valuation itself has none of that and is much better for it.
- The slideshow under the hero is mislabelled. There's a "Start" button and a "Hear Kira" button, and directly under both it says "Free · no sign-up · an indicative valuation on the spot." I clicked Start expecting the valuation. It turned into "Pause" — it's an autoplaying 13-slide story. Two clicks in, I've been told nothing and the caption under the button is describing a different thing entirely. Fix the caption or move it.
- Somewhere in that slideshow the copy slips from "you" into "his": the panel headed "Still only in his head" listing "How you price...". Small, but I notice when a thing about me suddenly talks about someone else.
- "Business Genome" is the one word I'd argue with. Genome is a science word. Nothing in my world is a genome. What you actually mean — and what your own example page proves — is "the operations manual your business never had." That phrase is already written on /genome. Use it.
- Opportunity: run the whole site through the test of "would a 66-year-old say this out loud to his accountant?" Genome fails it. "The manual the business never had" passes it, and it's your own line.

The free valuation — the questions
- This is the strongest part of the product and I'd say that to anyone. Eleven questions, three minutes, no email, no card, and it says so. I finished it, which is more than I've done with the two broker questionnaires that landed in my inbox last year.
- Question 8 — "If you took a 3-month holiday tomorrow, what happens?" — is the question my broker never asked me. That one question does more to establish you know this world than the whole homepage does.
- The industry question has a real problem. I typed "electric", the list offered "Electrical & Mechanical Contracting", and I clicked Next without picking it. It said "Checking 'electric' against our sector list…" and then just moved on to question 2. It never told me what it decided I was. That match sets the multiple, which sets the entire number. I only discovered it had matched me correctly because I pressed Back later and found the confirmation — "Matched to Electrical & Mechanical Contracting" — sitting on a screen I had already left. You wrote the confirmation and then showed it to nobody.
- The money fields don't format. I typed my turnover and it sits there as `2400000` — no commas, no "$2.4 million" echoed back — while the placeholder above it shows "e.g. 2,000,000". At my age, on a phone, `2400000` and `240000` are the same shape. If I fat-finger a zero, nothing on the screen tells me, and the first I'd know is a number that's out by a factor of ten. Format it as I type and echo it in words underneath.
- Question 3 asks for profit and then spends five lines explaining SDE, add-backs, interest, depreciation and one-offs. It's accurate and I got there, but it's the only screen in the flow where I had to read something twice. My bookkeeper could answer it in a second. Nobody offers to ask her.
- Opportunity: let me hand question 3 off. A "not sure — email these two questions to my bookkeeper" option, worded so it doesn't say why I'm asking, would remove the only hard question in the flow. It also solves a problem you don't seem to have noticed: the accurate number is in someone else's hands, and I can't ask for it without explaining myself.

The result
- Worth today $995,000. My broker told me to think about $600k. So the first thing this told me is that the man who wants to list my business has been low-balling me — which is exactly what I want to hear and exactly why I don't trust it. The tool that calculates my gap is the same tool whose monthly fee is a percentage of that gap. You say so plainly in the FAQ ("A bigger gap means more for her to unlock, so the bands move with it"), which is honest, but it means the bigger the number you show me, the more you charge. I noticed that within about ninety seconds. Anyone in my position will.
- The "where the multiples come from" note is the most honest paragraph I've read on a commercial website — US BizBuySell data, no Australian equivalent, "an indicative benchmark, not an Australian market quote". I respect it and it also cost you the sale. You've just told an Australian man that his Australian number comes from American data. I now have a number I like, that you've told me not to rely on, from a company that gets paid more if the number is bigger.
- Opportunity: this is the biggest strategic hole and it's fixable two ways. First, break the incentive link — price the product on business size or a flat band, not on the gap you calculated. Second, put one Australian anchor on the page: three or four de-identified local sale multiples in my trade with dates. You don't need a database, you need enough that the number stops being foreign.
- The four "where the value is hiding" cards were the part I actually read twice. "+$275,000 once captured" against owner dependence is specific enough to argue with, which is what makes it credible. Keep that.
- "Save / print this" — thank you. That is the correct button for a man who wants to take a piece of paper into the shed and not leave an account behind.

The signup gate — where I stopped
- The pricing card says, in a yellow badge: "Free while we are in beta — no card charged." Two inches below it, in the smallest grey type on the page, it says Stripe collects the card details and shows a "Sandbox" badge because you're in test mode. Then clicking through opens a box headed "The next screen asks for your card."
- So: no card charged, but you want my card. That's a distinction between "charged" and "collected" and I'm not inclined to give you the benefit of it. I'd rather you said, in the badge itself, "We take your card details now. Nothing is charged, and we'll email you before that changes."
- "That is our test mode, not a fake payment page" is the sentence that finished it. Nobody who wasn't suspicious is reassured by that line, and everybody who was is now more suspicious. Delete it. If you need to explain the Sandbox badge, explain it without using the word "fake".
- And underneath all of it: the word beta. You're asking me to put thirty-five years of how this business actually works into something that is still in test mode, with a sandbox badge on the payment page. I have one copy of what's in my head. I closed the tab here.
- Credit where it's due — the interstitial with "Continue to Stripe" / "Not yet" is exactly right. You told me what the next screen does before it did it, and "Not yet" backed me out cleanly without losing my valuation. More products should do that. The problem isn't the confirm; it's that the badge above it says the opposite.
- Opportunity: for this buyer, take the card out of the beta entirely. Email and a password, then ask for the card when billing actually turns on. You've already promised to email me first — so collect it then. The card is buying you nothing right now except the one objection that ends the conversation.

The Advisors page — the one I wish I hadn't clicked
- It's in the main menu, between "What you get" and "About", so I clicked it. It says: "Send us that client and we get the business out of their head... You introduce the client; you earn while they stay."
- I'm the client. I've just read a page, written for my broker, that explains he earns a trailing fee for sending me here. Whatever that page does for your channel, it costs you the owner who reads it — and this owner reads everything before he touches anything.
- Your privacy page handles the substance of this beautifully. "Never shared with anyone who referred you." "The handover document leaves out your own position — your plans, your circumstances, what you would accept." That last clause is the single most reassuring sentence on the entire site, because it shows you understand that in a sale, what I'd accept is the one thing that must never leak. It's buried in a legal page that most people won't open, while the page that undermines it is in the top nav.
- Opportunity: move the advisor channel behind its own entrance — a /for-advisors link in the footer, not in the owner's menu — and promote the "your position is never in the handover" promise up onto the landing page in your own words. You've solved the hardest trust problem in this market and you're hiding the solution under Privacy.

What you end up with — the page you've buried
- /genome is the best asset you have and I nearly never saw it. "Three builders supply roughly 60% of turnover... Hartley since 1998. None are on a written contract — work is allocated by a phone call to the owner, usually on a Friday." That's my business. That's the Friday phone call. Reading that did more for my belief that this thing works than everything else combined.
- It's in the nav as "What you get", which sounds like a feature list, so I skipped it on the way past. I only reached it from a text link on the pricing page, after the valuation, two steps past where I was already deciding whether to bother.
- Opportunity: rename it "See a real one" and put it in the hero next to the valuation button. Two doors, not one: "find out what it's worth" for the ones who want a number, "see what you end up with" for the ones like me who need to see the thing before they'll type anything. Right now the second door is behind the first.

Phone
- I checked at phone size because that's where I'd actually do this, in the ute at lunchtime. The valuation and the result both work properly — the three number cards stack, nothing runs off the side, the numbers are big enough to read.
- The top bar is jammed: logo, "Sign in" (which wraps onto two lines as "Sign / in"), a large green button and a hamburger, all fighting for 375 pixels. The wrapped "Sign in" looks like something is broken.
- A good deal of the explaining text on the homepage is small on the phone — the three "See the number / Capture your knowledge / Sell an asset" paragraphs and the captions under the buttons are all a size down from the body copy. On a laptop it's fine. Held at arm's length in daylight it isn't. That's not a preference; that's your entire stated audience.

Something nobody warned me about
- I came back to the valuation page later and it picked up exactly where I'd left off — question 11, all my answers still in. Convenient. It also means my turnover, my profit and "the business would struggle without me" are sitting in that browser for the next week, and anyone who opens that tab and presses Back four times reads them.
- I'm the man who hasn't told his wife. My bookkeeper uses my office computer. There is no warning, no "resume or start fresh?", no mention that anything was kept.
- To be fair, "Clear my answers" is on every screen, and I checked the privacy page — it's specific and it's good ("For most owners that is the most sensitive information they will type into anything, so it is worth being specific about it"). But the promise on the valuation screen is "It stays on this device with your answers", and the privacy page says "we hold what you entered only to produce the result". Both may be true; they don't read as the same statement, and I'm exactly the reader who will hold them side by side.
- Opportunity: on the resume, say so. "Picking up where you left off — your answers are saved in this browser for 7 days. Clear them?" And put one line of the privacy promise on the turnover screen itself, at the moment I'm deciding whether to type the number, not four clicks away.

Other Strategic Feature Suggestions
- Give me a number I can check. One line — "typical range for electrical contracting in Australia: 2.1× to 3.4× SDE" — with any source at all does more than three paragraphs of methodology honesty.
- Let me test the thing before I buy it. Ten minutes with Kira about one process — how I price a job — costs you nothing and would tell me more than the whole site. Right now the only way to find out whether she's any good is to give you a card.
- Say what happens when I die or sell. The Genome is the operations manual for a business I'm handing over. Who owns it at settlement, and can I hand it to the buyer as an asset? That's the question my solicitor will ask and it isn't answered anywhere.
- A print version of the Genome. Not because I'd read it on paper, but because a thing I can put in front of a buyer at a kitchen table is the actual product. "Save / print this" on the valuation was your best instinct on the whole site — extend it.
- Drop "beta" from the owner-facing copy before you go near this buyer. Say "we're taking a small first group and we'll tell you when billing starts" — same fact, and it doesn't sound like an experiment I'm the subject of.
- One voice, one palette. Pink buttons, purple numbers, green brand and emoji headings on a page about selling a life's work. The valuation flow and /genome already show you can do restrained. Make the rest match those two.

Standards Check (portfolio non-negotiables)
- Responsive 375 + 1440, no horizontal scroll: PASS — measured scrollWidth 375 at 375px on landing and result page, 1440 at 1440px; result cards stack cleanly on phone.
- Touch targets >=44px / body text >=16px on mobile: FAIL on text — header targets measure 44x44 and the CTA 110x60 (pass), but 15 blocks of substantive copy render at 14px on mobile, including the three "Built to sell" step explanations and every CTA caption.
- Nav collapses to usable mobile pattern: PASS — hamburger at 375px opens a drawer with all five nav items; "Sign in" wraps to two lines in the bar, cosmetic.
- Auth page: forgot-password + visibility toggle + magic link: PASS — /login has "Forgot password?", a "Show password" button, and "Email me a magic link". /admin/login has the same three.
- Authenticated chrome (persistent nav, /settings, Sign Out): N-A — I would not create an account, so I never got past auth. Cancellation copy refers to "Settings → Manage billing", which implies a settings page exists.
- Explanatory header on every page/panel: PASS — landing, /business-valuation, /plan, /genome, /advisors, /privacy and /login each open by saying what the page is and what to do.
- Voice agent reachable from chrome in <=3 clicks: FAIL on the public site — the only voice surface is "Hear Kira", which plays a pre-recorded mp3 (/demo-audio/...mp3) narrating a slideshow. No conversational voice widget renders on any public page. The product is sold as "you only ever talk to Kira" and there is nothing on the public site you can talk to.
- Browser tab title is the product name: PASS — "Kira — your part-time general manager" on the landing, "Sign in · Kira", "Privacy · Kira", "Terms · Kira".
- Dual-portal separation (user flow reaches a real user home, not an admin gate): PARTIAL — /admin correctly redirects to a separate /admin/login, and the user path (/login, /plan → Stripe → "You set your password and meet Kira right after") is distinct from it. I could not confirm the authenticated user home without an account.
- Consequence clarity + confirm on cost-incurring actions: PASS with a caveat — "The next screen asks for your card" with Continue/Not yet is exactly the right gate, but it directly contradicts the "no card charged" badge two inches above it.
- Zero dead ends: PASS — every screen offered the next move; "Not yet" backed out of checkout without losing the valuation; "Start over" and "Redo my valuation" both present.
- Content/IP acknowledgment: N-A — /terms and /privacy both exist, load, and contain no placeholder text; no acknowledgment gate reachable without an account.
- Address autocomplete / ABN lookup: N-A — no address or ABN field anywhere in the public flow. The industry field does have type-ahead with server-side matching.
