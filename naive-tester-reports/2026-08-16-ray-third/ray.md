# Third walkthrough — Ray, 66, electrical & mechanical contractor

Hi Dennis,

Third time. I said last time I'd stay in touch, and I meant it, so here I am again. I've
done the whole thing properly this time — took the valuation, made the account, told Kira
two real things about my business, and then went and read what she wrote as if I were about
to email it to a broker.

I'll give you the answer up front because you've earned that much: **this time I'd sign.**
Not because it's finished — it isn't, and I'll list what's wrong below — but because
somewhere in the middle of this I hit a screen that told me something about my own business
that I hadn't articulated to myself in thirty-five years. That's the first time that's
happened on any of these three visits. More on that at the end.

Screenshots are in `screens/`, and I've saved the actual handover document I'd have sent
someone as `handover-buyer-copy.html` so you can read it cold.

## 1. The way in, and the code you forgot you had

I clicked your link with the code in it. You told me, in a box I liked:

> *"Your invitation code is saved. We have kept WE6EDRWEF3WM and will use it when you get
> to the end — there is nothing to pay and no card to enter."*

Good. That's exactly what a suspicious man needs to hear. Then it sent me off to do the
valuation, which is fair enough, and when the valuation dropped me back at the plan page —
**the code was gone.** No banner. Instead I was looking at a $999/month checkout with a
"Start now" button and a card, and a small link at the bottom saying "Been invited to the
beta? Enter your invitation code." I clicked it. Empty box.

I had to go back to my email and find the code again. You explicitly promised me you'd kept
it. Two screens later you hadn't. That's the first thing on the whole visit and it's a
promise broken in under three minutes.

Worse: for the thirty seconds before I found that link, I was looking at a page asking me
for a card, on a product I'd been told was free to me. If I hadn't been the sort to poke
about, I'd have closed the tab thinking the offer was a bait-and-switch.

**Opportunity:** keep the banner on screen the whole way through, and once a code is in
hand, don't show the card checkout at all — show the redeem panel first and put the paid
plan below it. The code survives a round trip to the valuator either way; there's no reason
it can't survive back onto the page.

## 2. The valuation

This is the strongest thing you have and it has got better again.

$1.30m today, $1.51m captured, $210k in the gap. Walk-away as a range with the 40–60c
explanation. Then, and this is the bit that got me: **you told me why it was a single
number and not a range.** "Our sector data gives one median multiple and no spread around
it; putting a ± on them would be inventing a precision we do not have." Nobody says that.
Every valuer I've ever spoken to gives you a range because the range is where they hide.

The rent paragraph is worth the price of the whole exercise on its own. I own the yard
through the super fund and I have been charging the business under the odds for about a
decade because it suited my tax position. You're right that a buyer will re-work it, and
you're right that I'm carrying profit in there that isn't really profit. My accountant has
never once volunteered that. I'd want that paragraph printed and stapled to something.

Two things that snagged.

**The transferability score.** It says "Transferability score: 30/100" and then immediately
"0 means the business is you." Run together on screen it reads as **30/1000** for about a
second and a half. At 66 with reading glasses that's long enough to feel like a punch. Put
a full stop and a line break in there.

**"0.2 of a turn below."** I know what you mean now. I did not on first read. "A turn" is
broker language. You've been so careful everywhere else to write in plain English that this
one stood out — say "just under a fifth of a multiple below" or, better, "which is about
$95,000 of value."

**Opportunity:** you've got a $210k gap and a $999/month price on the same page. You do the
5.7% arithmetic on the plan page but not here. Put it here, where the emotion is: *"closing
this gap costs about 5.7% of it per year, and it stops after twelve months."* That's the
sentence that turns a number into a decision.

## 3. Setting Kira up

The account went in fine. Password, tickbox, terms and privacy both real links, in.

Then the setup screen went **jet black**. Not dark-mode-black — a black panel with orange
buttons, in the middle of a product that has been cream-and-white for twenty minutes. It is
the third distinct-looking piece of software I've been in since I clicked your link
(cream marketing site → grey-and-white app → black setup). I genuinely paused to check I
hadn't landed somewhere else. See `screens/09-draft-top.png` next to `screens/11-genome-1.png`.

**"Create My Kira"** — the capital M is doing nothing for you. It reads like a menu command.
"Set her up" is what your own heading two lines above already says, and it's better.

**The "Listening..." spinner.** On the setup page there's a small spinner reading
"Listening..." that was on the whole time and never resolved — including on the *typed*
brief form, where nothing is listening to anything. See `screens/08-typebrief.png`. On a
product whose entire pitch to me is "she's recording what I say," a permanent
"Listening..." with no way to stop it is not a cosmetic bug. I looked at it twice.

**Two different faces.** The avatar at the top of the setup page and the big picture in the
card below it are not the same photograph — one's wearing a headset and one isn't, and the
faces differ. If she's a person, she has one face.

**Opportunity:** the typed-brief route worked well and I'd guess more of your ICP will use
it than you think. But it's presented as the failure path — "No microphone, or prefer to
type?" in small underlined text below a greyed-out button. Give it equal billing. Half of us
are in a truck with the radio on, and the other half don't want the office hearing.

## 4. Talking to her

I typed her two real things: how I price, and who Wayne and Karen actually are.

She took both accurately. Read them back correctly. Got the rates right, got the July
review right, got the permit signing right. No hallucination, nothing invented. That's not
nothing — it's the thing I was most worried about.

But she didn't **do** anything with them. Both times the reply was a tidy summary followed
by *"Want me to capture more on pricing or take it from here?"*

Dennis, I just told her that the only other man on earth who can sign a permit on my biggest
client's site is 61, has no written contract, and will walk out the day I do. That is the
single largest hole in my business. A buyer's advisor hearing that stops the meeting. She
said "Want to cover more on your key people?"

You sell her as *"asks the questions you haven't thought of"* and *"pushes back when
something is unclear."* On the live conversation she does neither. She files.

**Opportunity:** you already have the pushback engine — it's in "Check this area" (see §7).
It's just not in the conversation. When she takes a fact that trips a known risk (sole
signatory, no contract, key-person, single client over 30%), have her say so on the spot:
*"Before we go on — is Wayne's permit authority written into the mine's contract, or is it
personal to him? That one answer is worth more than most of this."* That's the moment I'd
tell my mates about.

## 5. Coming back to the Genome

**It moves.** Pricing went to "Building up", "Who does the work" filled three segments,
"Money in, money out" two. So the answer to your question is yes, and yes — what she
captured reads like it's about *my* business, in my numbers, not about the software.

Two problems.

**It took several minutes and told me nothing while it did.** I finished talking, went
straight to My Genome, and everything said "Nothing yet" with all nine bars empty. I read
the whole page. She had just told me she'd got it. The page said she hadn't. I only found
out it works because I came back later for an unrelated reason. Most owners will not come
back later — they'll conclude it doesn't work, which is exactly what I concluded for about
twenty minutes. Put a line on the page: *"She's still filing the last conversation — check
back in a few minutes."*

**"0 of 9 well covered so far."** That sentence sits directly above a grid where three of
the nine visibly have green in them. I understand what you mean — "well covered" is a high
bar. But the headline number a man reads is zero, and the picture underneath says three.
Show both: *"3 areas started, 0 well covered."* Otherwise the summary contradicts the
picture and the picture is the honest one.

**The key under the grid** — "1 you mentioned it · 2 she has the basics · 3 most of it is
written down · 4 a buyer could use it" — is there, and the wording is genuinely good. But
it's four bare numbers in a row of text. Nothing connects them to the four stacked blocks in
the bars. I had to work out that the bars fill bottom-up and that block 1 is the bottom one.
Draw a tiny four-block bar next to the key, filling as it goes, and it's instant.

**The privacy paragraph says the same thing twice inside itself:** "...never shared with
anyone you have referred or been referred by, and it is never shown to a buyer" and then two
sentences later "It is never shared with anyone who referred you, and never shown to a
buyer." I noticed because I was reading that paragraph *carefully*, which is the whole point
of it being there.

## 6. The area pages

Yes — there's something to do on every one of the nine, on a brand new account. That's fixed
and it's a real improvement on last time. One button, clearly labelled, no dead ends.

One thing grates. Every area page shows me the buyer's-advisor question in the third person:

- *"Could someone else reach **his** number?"*
- *"Does the work happen without **him** on site?"*
- *"...are supplier terms personal to **him**?"*

Whose number? It's my number. I know why it's like that — you've written the buyer's copy of
the document in third person, correctly, and these strings are shared. But I'm reading it in
*my* app, and it reads as though the software is discussing me with someone else. Given
everything else on the page is about how private this is, that's a bad note to strike. The
owner copy of the document already has the second-person version of every one of these
questions written — use those in the app.

## 7. "Check this area" — the best thing you've built

I nearly missed this. On a fresh area it says "Not checked yet" with no explanation of what
checking is, so I ignored it. After I'd talked to her, the same box said *"Kira holds 4
things about this part of your business"* and offered a button. I pressed it.

What came back:

> **On the record (2)** — the actual rates or margins, written down ✓ · when prices last
> moved and what triggered it ✓
>
> **Needs a better answer (2)** — *"When a job comes in, how do you actually arrive at the
> price?"* The record lists cost-plus margins and hourly rates but does not provide a full
> method someone else could follow... *"When a job is unusual, what happens to the price —
> and who makes that call?"* The record mentions a 10% contingency but does not specify who
> decides.
>
> **Not yet (2)** — can anyone else discount, and how far? · where does the price list
> actually live? — *"Worth having, but a buyer will not stop over it."*

Dennis. **That second one is right and I have never said it out loud.** Nobody but me makes
that call. That's why I can't take three weeks off in shutdown season and I have blamed the
clients for that for a decade. It's not the clients.

And "Worth having, but a buyer will not stop over it" is the most useful sentence in your
product, because it tells me what *not* to spend my Sunday on. Every consultant I've ever
paid gave me a list of forty things and no ranking.

**This is the product.** Everything else is plumbing that gets me here.

**Opportunity:** it is three clicks deep, behind a button that means nothing until after
you've talked, on a page most people won't open. Surface it. When she's captured enough in
an area, put the check on the Overview: *"I've got enough on pricing to check it against
what a buyer asks — want me to?"* And say what checking does *before* I've done it, so the
"Not checked yet" box on an empty area is an invitation rather than a shrug.

## 8. The handover document — read as the thing I'd send a broker

I opened it as instructed, and I read it as if Neil (the broker who told me I'm worth less
than I think) had it in his inbox.

**The good.** It opens with no login, it's plain and printable, every line is dated, and the
buyer's copy correctly leaves out the fact that I'm thinking of selling — I checked, and my
setup brief with "eventually sell it" in it is absent from the buyer version and present in
my own. The two-file split works. That was the thing I most wanted to verify and it holds.

**The bad, and it's bad enough to stop me sending it.**

Under **"Who does the work"** there are four bullets from one paragraph I typed:

1. "Neither Wayne nor Karen have written contracts; their employment is based on handshake agreements."
2. "Karen manages all invoicing and BAS, and works without a written contract on a handshake agreement."
3. "Wayne has led shutdown crews for 19 years, is the only other person authorised to sign off mine site permits, and is expected to leave when the owner leaves."
4. "Wayne is the leading hand with 19 years at the company, running every shutdown crew and the only other person allowed by the mine to sign off on permits. Karen handles all invoicing and BAS tasks. Neither have written contracts; they're employed on a handshake. Wayne is 61 and is expected to leave when the owner does."

Bullet 4 contains bullets 1, 2 and 3 in full. Karen's handshake appears in **three** of the
four. And then it appears **a fourth time** under "Money in, money out and terms", same
sentence again. The word "handshake" is in that document five times, from one paragraph I
wrote once. See `handover-buyer-copy.html`.

I'd be embarrassed to send that. It doesn't read as thorough, it reads as though nobody
proofed it — and the man reading it is already looking for reasons to discount me.

**Notes about the software, in the document.** Bullet 4 is followed by **"source not
recorded"**. And my own copy has a heading "Recorded, not yet filed — Said, kept, and not
yet placed in an area", and under it *"What he said he wants to work on, in his own words
when he set me up..."*. That's Kira writing about her own filing process in a document
you've told me is mine to keep forever. Take it out of the document; keep it on the screen
if you need it.

**The count is right** — buyer copy says 9 entries, my copy says 10, and there are 10 in the
app. Credit where it's due; I went looking for a mismatch and didn't find one.

**Two names for one thing.** The app calls it "the handover document" everywhere. The
document calls itself "Operating manual". Pick one.

**No business name.** The document is titled **"Ray"**. That's my first name. A broker
receives a document called Ray. It should be titled with the trading name, with my name
under it as the source.

**Opportunity:** the fix for the duplication is not clever. Before writing an entry, ask
whether it's already contained in an existing one — and if she's about to file a long
paragraph *and* three sentences pulled out of it, file the sentences and drop the paragraph.
Four bullets that say the same thing is worse than one that says it once.

## 10. Settings, and the money

Settings is good and I want to say so plainly. Profile, business details, connected
accounts, plan and usage, password, notifications, sign out everywhere, delete account. All
of it with a sentence saying what it's for.

> *"There's no card on your account, so nothing can be charged."*
> *"$0.00 of $20 included. That is the cost of talking to Kira... It is included, not billed
> to you, and it resets each month."*

That's the right way round. You've told me what it costs *you*, told me it's not my problem,
and told me where I stand. I have never seen a software company do that and it did more for
my trust than any of the marketing.

The billing story generally is the most honest I've read: billed in arrears, three days'
warning, cancel and the month's on us, a third of the rate after twelve months whether the
work's done or not, and every price carrying "+ GST". You've clearly thought hard about
being trusted by people like me, and it lands.

## Scope — what I couldn't reach

- **No microphone.** Every conversation was through the text box on `/talk`, and I said so.
  I have not heard her voice, judged her pacing, or tested whether she interrupts. For a
  product whose pitch is "you talk," that's a large unchecked area and it's the one I'd want
  most before signing a twelve-month commitment.
- **Never paid.** I came in on an invitation code, so no card was taken. I have not seen
  Stripe checkout, the arrears invoice, the three-day warning email, or a cancellation.
  Everything in §10 is what the screen *says*, not what happens.
- **No email received or checked.** I don't know whether anything actually arrived.
- **One conversation, two facts, three areas.** Six of the nine areas are still empty and I
  never ran "Check this area" on more than one. I don't know whether the quality of that
  check holds up on people, cash or compliance.
- **Google Drive not connected**, so I never saw the filing-into-my-own-Drive behaviour you
  advertise on the landing page — which is one of the two or three things that most
  distinguishes you.
- **Didn't test Share** on the Genome, or the advisor/referrer view.
- **Your browser tooling fell over on me repeatedly** during the session — I got signed out
  several times mid-walk and had to log back in. I've assumed that's your test rig rather
  than the product, and I haven't counted it as a finding, but I mention it because a couple
  of my "it logged me out" moments may have been that and not you.

## Would I sign?

**Yes.** With one condition.

Twice before I said "not yet, but keep me posted," and both times the honest reason was that
I couldn't see what I'd be getting for a thousand dollars a month. I can now. It's not the
valuation — the valuation is a good hook and I'd have paid a couple of hundred for it once.
It's that "Check this area" screen, which read my own words back to me and told me the thing
I've spent ten years not saying: *nobody but me decides what happens when a job goes
sideways.* If it does that to me nine times, I'd pay $999 a month for a year without
blinking. That's about half a week of Wayne's time and it might be the difference between
$1.3m and $1.5m.

**The one thing standing in the way is the document.**

Because here's what I'd actually do with this. I'd work with her for six months, and then
I'd send the handover document to Neil the broker, and to my accountant, and probably to my
daughter. That document *is* the product — everything else is the machine that makes it. And
right now the document has one paragraph of mine turned into four overlapping bullets, the
word "handshake" five times, a line reading "source not recorded", a heading about your own
filing system, and my first name where the business's name should be.

I would not send that to Neil. He's already told me I'm worth less than I think. Handing him
a document that repeats itself four times would confirm exactly the impression I'm paying
you to change.

Fix the document — dedupe it, take your own housekeeping notes out of it, and put the
business's name on the front — and I'll give you my card. Everything else on this list I'd
live with.

The other thing I'd say, and take it as encouragement rather than a complaint: **stop asking
me to do the valuation twice.** Of everything here, that's the one that made me wonder
whether the left hand knows what the right is doing, and it's a morning's work.

You're close.

Ray
