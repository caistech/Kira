# Kira — a walkthrough by someone you built this for

Hi Dennis,

**Persona:** Ray, 66. Electrical contractor, 35 years, 9 staff, ~$3.2m turnover. Thinking about
selling. Haven't told my staff, my kids, and some weeks not my wife.
**URL:** https://kiraexec.com (production, f7e2f2d)
**Goal:** Decide whether this is worth my time, and run the valuation through to the number.
No account, no card.
**Duration:** ~75 minutes, 1440px then 375px.

I'll say up front: the writing on this thing is better than anything I've been sent by a broker,
an accountant or a business coach in five years. Somebody has actually sat with a man like me. That
makes the failures below more annoying, not less — because they're the kind that make me close the
tab on something that was about to work.

---

## The landing page

- **"You spent thirty years building it. Now sell it for what it's actually worth."** That's the
  right sentence. And underneath it, "a buyer isn't buying an asset, they're buying you, and they
  price it accordingly" — that's the thing the broker said to me in a way that made me want to
  throw him out, and here it's said in a way I can hear. Whoever wrote this: don't let anyone
  "optimise" it.
- **"Most owners start this before they've told anyone."** Eleven words that told me you know who
  you're talking to. I read the rest of the page because of that line.
- Three numbers sit halfway down — **WALK AWAY $220k / TODAY $684k / CAPTURED $879k** — with no
  explanation of what "walk away" means. Walk away from what? From the sale? From the business? I
  had to get all the way into the valuation before I found out it means the auction value of your
  gear. That definition exists, word-perfect, on the valuation intro page. It should be on the
  landing, because right now the most striking numbers on the page are the ones I can't read.
- At 1440 the whole page lives in a column on the left and the right half is empty. I don't mind
  white space, but combined with the nav wrapping onto two lines ("How it / works", "See a real /
  one", "Create an / account") it reads slightly unfinished. Not a dealbreaker; a first impression.

**Opportunity:** Put one line under the three numbers — "walk away = what the gear fetches at
auction; today = what a buyer pays for a business that needs you; captured = what it's worth once
it doesn't." Ten seconds of work, and it turns three mystery numbers into the argument.

## "Ask Kira anything — no account needed" — the part that decided it for me

This is where the run turned. I want to walk through it exactly.

- The copy says: *"No account, no card, nothing saved to your business… If you would rather type,
  you can."* Good. I'm not talking out loud to a computer in an office with a bookkeeper twenty feet
  away. Typing is the only version of this I will ever use.
- **There is no text box.** There's a black button with a microphone on it that says "Talk to the
  assistant." So the sentence offering me typing is sitting above a control that offers me the one
  thing I won't do. I clicked it because the copy promised typing, not because I wanted to talk.
- I clicked, and got "Mute / End" — it was trying to put me on a call. **Fourteen seconds** passed
  before the typing box appeared. I know you know about this delay. I'm mentioning it because it
  genuinely affected me: for those fourteen seconds the page was asking me to speak, and I had my
  hand on the tab close button.
- I typed the only question a man in my position asks first:

  > *"If I tell you my turnover, who else can see it? My staff don't know I'm thinking of selling."*

- The box cleared. Nothing appeared where I'd typed. I looked at that empty box for a good while
  and assumed it had gone nowhere.
- **The answer was in the opposite corner of the screen.** Bottom-right, a fixed little card,
  340 pixels below and 800 across from where my eyes were. On a 1440 screen that is not "next to
  the thing you did", that's a different part of the room. I only found it when I scrolled down to
  read something else.
- And here is what it said:

  > *"Got it. Voice needs a microphone and this browser has not given one, so your question has
  > gone to a person rather than to Kira. **Dennis reads these himself** and will come back to you."*

Three problems, in ascending order of how much they cost you:

1. **I never gave you an address.** You can't come back to me. So either the sentence is untrue, or
   you have some way of reaching me I wasn't told about — and for me, right now, the second reading
   is the frightening one.
2. **A named human being now knows I'm selling.** Not a machine. A man with a name, who I've never
   met. That is precisely the thing I have spent eight months not doing.
3. **Your own page says the opposite.** Scroll up forty pixels from where that card appeared and
   slide 3 of your walkthrough is on screen at the same moment, saying in bold:

   > *"What I cannot do: no background listening, no reading your email, and **nobody at our end
   > reads your conversations**."*

   Both sentences were visible in the same browser window at the same time. I've kept the
   screenshot (`26-carousel-after-start.png`). One of them is wrong and I don't know which, and the
   one that reassures me is the one I now can't believe.

I understand the distinction you'd draw — a question typed into a marketing widget isn't "a
conversation with Kira". I don't. I asked a private question and was told a person reads it, on a
page that told me nobody does.

**Opportunity:** the honest version is short and would have kept me. *"No microphone here, so I
can't answer live. If you leave an email I'll reply myself — it's me, Dennis, one person, and I
won't put you on a list. Or read the FAQ below, which covers this."* Name the human, collect the
address, and either fix the slide 3 wording or scope it ("nobody reads your Genome conversations").
And put the reply **in the box I typed into.**

## The thirteen-slide walkthrough

- Slide 1: *"You have thought about what comes next, and probably told nobody. And underneath it:
  after thirty years, the thing you would be selling is mostly you."* I sat with that one.
- Slide 13: *"nobody has to know you looked."* Right note to end on.
- Thirteen is a lot. It auto-plays at about seven seconds a slide, so that's a minute and a half of
  me watching illustrations. The illustrations are the weak part — a beige rectangle with a stick
  table on slide 1, a phone outline with coloured bars on slide 3. They look like the placeholder

- "Start" and "Hear Kira" sit side by side as equals and I couldn't tell what either would do.

**Opportunity:** cut it to six and put real photographs behind them — a ute, a whiteboard, a bloke
in a shed. The copy has earned better pictures than it's got.

## "See a real one" — the example Genome

This is the best page on the site and I'd lead with it.

- *"Three builders supply roughly 60% of turnover. All three came through the owner personally;
  Hartley since 1998. None are on a written contract — work is allocated by a phone call to the
  owner, usually on a Friday."* That is my business with the names changed. Whoever wrote that has
  been in a room with a contractor.
- **"Still only in your head — why the fourth builder stopped calling in 2023."** That's the moment
  I understood what you're selling. Not a document. A list of the things I've never written down
  and know I should have.
- **Every area badge says "documented", including the empty one.** Nine cards down the page, and
  the last one reads:

  > **Who buys, and who owns the relationship** — **Empty** / *documented*

  Empty and documented. Which is it? And that card still shows a green progress bar with something
  in it. It's on every card regardless of state, so it's obviously a label that got left on, but I
  read it as the page contradicting itself twice on the same line.
- **Only the first of nine areas actually opens.** The other eight are one-line summaries. I tried
  clicking "Who does the work" — the one that matters most to me, because it's the question of who
  walks out the door the day I announce — and nothing happens. They're not clickable. So "see a
  real one" shows me one ninth of a real one.
- The Privacy-mode section is honest and I respect it, but read what it promises: *"Kira sits
  quietly in the background while you work and only wakes when you say her name."* For a man whose
  entire problem is leakage, "sits quietly in the background" is not a feature, it's the thing I'm
  afraid of. You immediately say it isn't built. Good. But the sentence before that lands first.

**Opportunity:** make all nine areas open. It costs you nothing, it's already written for one of
them, and "what's still only in his head" repeated nine times is the most persuasive thing you own.

## Pricing

- **"$499 + GST to $4,999 + GST /month"** — GST stated, both ends of the range. Thank you. I can't
  tell you how many quotes I get that don't.
- **"Which band you land in depends on the size of your business — the annual profit you tell us,
  not the gap we calculate."** That's a genuinely clever thing to say and I believed it. You then
  proved it: I entered $460k of profit and got quoted $999. It's tied to the profit, not the gap.
  Credit where it's due.
- **But you won't tell me how long.** *"It is meant to end… We will tell you when you get there; we
  do not put a date on it."* I'm 66 and I want out inside two years. $999 + GST a month with no
  stated end is an open cheque, and no man my age signs one of those. You have enough owners through
  this to say "for a business your size, typically nine to fourteen months — call it $10k to $14k
  plus GST." Say that and I can take it to my wife. Refuse to, and I can't.

**Opportunity:** publish a typical duration band per plan tier, with the caveat you already use
elsewhere ("it depends on how much is still only in your head"). The refusal to estimate reads as
evasion, when I think it's actually caution.

## The About page

I clicked this because I wanted to know who I'd be handing my books to. It undid a lot of work.

- The rest of the site is cream and dark green, sober, no decoration. **This page is navy and
  orange with a pink-to-orange gradient wordmark and a yellow pill button.** I genuinely thought
  I'd clicked through to a different company.
- Four emoji: 👨‍💻 🎯 🗣️ 🔧. Nowhere else on the site has one.
- The language: *"a suite of AI Voice Agent platforms"*, *"a marketplace of specialized AI Voice
  Agents"*, *"Longtail AI Ventures"*, *"The thesis behind everything we build"*, *"That's the bet.
  That's the vision. And we're just getting started."* Every one of those is the noise I came to
  this site to get away from. The version of this on your own landing page — *"thirty years in
  construction, logistics and modular manufacturing, running the businesses, not advising them"*,
  *"one developer I work with is eighty, has built a substantial land bank, and none of it is
  written down anywhere but in his head"* — is worth more than this entire page. That paragraph
  made me trust you. This page took it back.
- **"Voice-First — Designed for natural conversation. Talk, don't type."** Your landing page says
  "if you would rather type, you can." This one tells me not to.
- **"Always Improving — Every agent learns, adapts, and gets better with every conversation."** I
  read that as: you train on what I tell you. Your landing page explicitly says my details are
  *"not sold, not pooled, not used to train anyone's model."* I know you probably mean two
  different things. I can't tell which one to believe.
- **Two footers, stacked, with two different years** — "© 2025 Corporate AI Solutions" directly
  above "© 2026 Corporate AI Solutions". Small thing. It's the kind of small thing I notice on an
  invoice.
- Four links on this page go to **`corporate-ai-solutions.vercel.app`** — a raw hosting address. If
  I click "A Corporate AI Solutions Product" and land on something dot vercel dot app, I've learned
  the parent company doesn't have a website.

**Opportunity:** delete this page and replace it with the "Who is behind this" section from your
landing page, at full length, on the same cream background. You already wrote the right About page.
It's just on the wrong URL.

## The link that would have ended it on its own

In the footer of **every page** is "corporateaisolutions.com". I clicked it, because that's what a
suspicious man does before he types a turnover figure into a website.

That domain **serves a certificate issued to `*.ingress-earth.ewp.live`** — somebody's hosting
platform, not your company. A browser puts a full red "Your connection is not private" page in
front of it. Behind the warning, it 404s.

So: the company name at the bottom of the page I'm being asked to trust with my life's work throws
a security warning. That is a five-minute fix and it is the single most expensive broken thing on
this site, because it converts "cautious bloke doing his homework" into "closed the tab" with no
message and no clue for you that it happened.

## The valuation — eleven questions

This is the best-built thing here, and I'd have paid attention to whoever wrote the question copy.

- The intro says exactly what I needed on the landing: three numbers explained, *"About 3 minutes.
  Nothing to sign up for"*, and then **"Your answers are kept on this device as you go, so you can
  stop and come back — for 7 days, on this device only. Nothing is sent anywhere until you decide
  to sign up."** That paragraph is why I kept going. It's the only place on the site that answers
  the question I asked the chat box.
- **Q1, the industry lookup, has a ranking problem.** I typed what I call myself — *"electrical
  contracting"* — and the only thing offered was **"Residential Building & General Contracting"**.
  I'm not a builder. My multiple isn't a builder's multiple. It was only when I shortened it to
  "electrical" that "Electrical & Mechanical Contracting" appeared. So typing the *more* accurate
  description gets you the *less* accurate answer, and the right category is invisible. Given the
  page tells me the sector "sets the multiple, so if it is wrong the number is too", that matters.
  (Credit though: "sheet metal" correctly found Metal Products. The synonym matching is good; the
  ranking on multi-word phrases isn't.)
- **Q2/Q3 are excellently written.** "Total sales — everything the business invoices or takes in
  over a year, before any costs come out. We ask about profit on the next screen." Then the profit
  question defines SDE properly and gives a worked example with real numbers. I could do that
  without ringing my accountant, which is the whole point.
- **The profit guard is the best single piece of design here.** I deliberately typed my turnover
  into the profit box, the way a tired man would at nine at night. It came back: *"That's a 100%
  margin on the $3,200,000 turnover you entered. Looks right? Remember this figure includes your own
  salary and perks added back."* It caught me, didn't block me, and didn't talk to me like an idiot.
  Whoever fought for that: you were right.
- **Q8 — "If you took a 3-month holiday tomorrow, what happens?"** is the question my broker never
  asked and the one that actually decides the price. Putting it in a free three-minute tool is the
  smartest thing on this site.
- Small ones: "annual **PROFIT**?" in shouting capitals — I can read. And it echoed my figure back
  as **"$460 thousand"**, which no human writes. $460k or $460,000.

**Four things you never asked me, and a buyer will:**

1. **Debt.** I've got about $380k of vehicle and equipment finance. You disclaim it at the bottom
   of the results — *"subtract any loans, equipment finance, lease obligations or tax owing"* — and
   make me do the arithmetic in my head, when you could have asked in one box and shown me the
   number I actually care about, which is what lands in my pocket.
2. **The premises.** I own the yard through my super fund. For a trade business that's often the
   biggest single question in the deal, and it's not here at all.
3. **Work in progress and retentions.** For a contractor that's real money sitting on other
   people's balance sheets.
4. **When I want out.** The entire product is aimed at a man in his sixties, and never once asks
   how long he's got. Two years and eight years are completely different advice.

**Opportunity:** one more screen — "roughly what do you owe, and do you own the premises?" — turns
"an indicative valuation of the business" into "roughly what you'd walk away with." That's the
number I'd screenshot and show my wife.

## The result page

**The numbers, first, because I checked them:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Walk away | $136,000 – $204,000 |
| Worth today | $1,290,000 (~2.8× SDE) |
| With knowledge captured | $1,490,000 (~3.2× SDE) |
| The gap | $200,000 |
| Transferability | 30/100 |

- **The arithmetic ties.** 1,490,000 − 1,290,000 = 200,000, and the four drivers underneath —
  owner dependence $81,400, documented systems $69,000, recurring revenue $30,200, client
  diversification $19,400 — add to exactly $200,000. I added them up with a pen. They're right.
  That's rarer than you'd think and it's the reason I read the rest.
- **"Why the number is what it is" is the best writing on the site.** *"It is the same as buying a
  car sight unseen on the seller's promises: you would knock the price down to cover the unknown
  unknowns, because you are the one who wears it if things turn out worse than described."* That is
  the broker's argument, explained in a way that doesn't insult me, and it's the first time anyone
  has made me think he might have had a point rather than just been lowballing me.
- **The honesty about ranges is unusual and I noticed it.** *"The two business figures are single
  numbers because our sector data gives one median multiple and no spread around it; putting a ± on
  them would be inventing a precision we do not have."* Nobody says that. Say it more.

**Now the problem with the argument.**

- **You keep referring to my sector's average multiple and never tell me what it is.** *"which is
  why the multiple sits below your sector's average, not at it."* Below by how much? If electrical
  contracting averages 4.1× and I'm at 2.8×, that's a fact I'd chew on for a week. As written it's
  a claim I can't check, on a page whose whole credibility rests on being checkable.
- **The uplift is smaller than the pitch.** Capturing everything in my head moves me from 2.8× to
  3.2× — **15.5%**. Meanwhile the plumbing example on your landing page goes $684k → $879k, which is
  **28.5%**. So the marketing example is nearly twice as flattering as what the calculator gave me.
  And by your own page's language, 3.2× is still nowhere near the 5× top of your stated range. I'm
  told "sell an asset, not a job", and the tool tells me that after doing all the work I'm at 3.2×
  and still, in your words, closer to selling a job.
- **$200,000 against $999 + GST a month, for an unstated number of months.** That's a good ratio if
  it's ten months. It's a bad one at thirty. See the pricing section — this is where the missing
  duration estimate actually costs you the sale, because this is the page where I'm doing the sum.
- **"SDE" appears twice on this page and is never expanded here.** It's defined back on question 3,
  which I can no longer see. This is the page with a "Save / print this" button on it — the page I'd
  hand to my accountant. Spell it out where it's printed.
- **The printout has no date and no business name on it.** If I print this and put it in a drawer,
  in six months I won't know what turnover figure it was based on or when I ran it. Put the
  inputs, the date and the sector in a small block at the bottom. That single change turns it from a
  screenshot into a document.
- I clicked "Save / print this" and it opened my browser's print dialog, which sat over the page
  and locked it up for a couple of minutes. Might be my machine. But "Save" in the label suggests a
  file, and what I got was a printer.

**And then the thing I can't unsee.** After ten screens of sober cream, plain type and no
decoration — which is exactly why I trusted it — the results page opens with **a second Kira logo
in pink-and-orange gradient, a bright pink rule, a sparkle emoji next to "Your indicative
valuation", and my gap number in white on a purple-to-magenta gradient card.** It's the visual
language of a product being sold to me, arriving at the precise moment I was reading a number about
my own retirement. I don't distrust the number because of it. But something in me sat back.

**Opportunity:** state the sector average multiple next to mine, and put the date, sector and my
four input figures in a footer block on the printed version. And leave the results page the same
colour as the rest of the site — the sobriety is the product.

- **Two headers.** `/genome`, `/business-valuation` and `/plan` each ship two `<header>` elements —
  the green "K Kira" bar, then a second pink "Kira" wordmark underneath. On my phone that's about
  15% of the screen spent on the same logo twice before any words appear.
- **`/about` ships two footers** with two different copyright years.
- **`/plan`** — the page behind "Start building your Business Genome", i.e. the page where you ask
  for money — arrives from your server as a heading, one paragraph, and the words **"Reading your
  valuation from this device…"**. Everything else, including the price, gets drawn afterwards by the
  browser. I couldn't measure how long that takes because my browser fell over, so I'm not claiming
  it's slow. I am saying that of every page on this site, the one that starts as a loading message
  is the one where I'm deciding to pay you.

## Scope

About 75 minutes. Covered: landing at 1440 and 375, the Ask-Kira box (typed, submitted, read the
reply), the thirteen-slide walkthrough, the example Genome, the pricing section, the FAQ accordions,
the About page, and the full eleven-question valuation through to the result page, including
deliberately mis-entering the profit to test the guard.

**Could not reach:** anything behind signup or payment (won't do either); the actual voice
conversation (no microphone on this machine, which is how I ended up in the text box in the first
place); `/plan` rendered in a browser and the results page at 375px — my browser died after the
print dialog and wouldn't come back. **Not verified:** how long `/plan` shows its loading message;
whether "Clear my answers" asks before it clears; the mobile results page.

## VERDICT: **FAIL**

Not on the product. The valuation engine, the question copy, the profit guard and the "why the
number is what it is" essay are the best I've seen aimed at people like me, and the arithmetic
checks out to the dollar.

It fails on trust, and on the two places where trust is decided:

1. **A page that says "nobody at our end reads your conversations" while, in the same browser
   window, telling me a named man reads what I just typed — and promising to come back to me
   without taking an address.** I asked the one question that matters to me and got a contradiction
   for an answer.
2. **The company's own domain, linked in the footer of every page, throws a browser security
   warning and then 404s.**

Either one on its own closes the tab, and you'd never know, because men like me don't send an email
about it. Fix those two and the About page, and I'd have come back and read it again with my wife.
