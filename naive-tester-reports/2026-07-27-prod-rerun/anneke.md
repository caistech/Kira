## User Path — the free valuation

This is the part I came to judge, and it is much better than it was this morning.

**The SDE wording is fixed.** Question 3 now reads: *"What's left after all costs, plus the salary and perks you pay yourself (often called SDE). Not turnover — the smaller number you actually keep, with your own pay added back."* And underneath: *"If the business kept $150k after costs and paid you $50k in salary and perks, add them together and enter $200,000."* That is correct, it's in plain English, and the worked example does the heavy lifting. I'd have no problem with a client reading that.

- **But it's still an incomplete SDE.** Proper seller's discretionary earnings adds back interest, depreciation and amortisation and any genuine one-off costs, not just the owner's pay. My test business is a plumbing outfit with a yard of vans. Depreciation on six vehicles and a compressor fleet is real money — an owner following your instruction literally will hand you a number 10–20% light, and that error walks straight through the multiple into the headline. One more clause: *"and add back interest, depreciation and any one-off costs that won't repeat."*
- The questions themselves are genuinely advisory. Client concentration (Q7), owner dependence (Q8), where the processes live (Q9), locked-in revenue (Q10) — that is the same list I work through on a whiteboard. Q8's framing, *"the single biggest driver of what your business is worth, and the thing most owners never think about until they try to sell"*, is exactly right.
- I tried to break it. Typed "alpaca stud farm" into the industry field: *"No sector match for 'alpaca'. You can carry on — we'll use the overall market-average multiple — but a closer match gives a better number."* Honest, doesn't block me, tells me what it's costing me. Good.
- I entered $200,000 turnover and $600,000 profit: *"That's higher than the turnover you entered ($200,000). Profit is what you keep after costs, so it should be lower than turnover — did you mean to enter sales here?"* Caught it, explained it, didn't lecture. Good.
- **The walk-away number is the one optimistic figure on an otherwise conservative page.** I entered $220,000 of gear, vehicles and stock, and "WALK AWAY" came back as $220,000 exactly. That's not a walk-away, that's a book value. Sell six vans and a workshop at auction on a four-week timeline and you'll see 40–60 cents in the dollar, before you've paid out staff, made good the lease or chased the last of your debtors. Discount it, or rename it "asset value". As it stands it's the only number on that page a buyer's accountant would laugh at.
- **You never ask about debt.** The disclaimer at the bottom does say the figure is before debt and lists loans, equipment finance and lease obligations. But it's below the fold, after the fireworks. An owner with $400k of chattel mortgage sees $690,499 in 48-point type and files it as his number. One question — "roughly what does the business owe?" — and you could show him the equity figure, which is the one he'll actually retire on.
- The uplift breakdown is well presented, but two of the four components came back at exactly $75,818 (documented systems, and recurring revenue). Identical to the dollar reads as a fixed weighting rather than anything about my business, and an advisor will spot it in about four seconds.
- **The methodology note is the best thing on the site.** You state that the multiples come from BizBuySell's 2025 US data, about 9,500 closed deals, ~2.5× SDE market average; that there is no equivalent Australian dataset at that granularity; that the AIBB's database is members-only; and that Australian brokers lean on the same US data for that reason. Then you say the sector shape travels but the absolute number should be checked locally. I have not seen a free online valuation tool do that, ever. It is the single reason I'd be willing to put this in front of a client at all — you've told them where the number came from and how much to trust it.
- Small things: the auto-advance after picking an option takes two to three seconds with no "saving" feedback — on a phone in a ute, an owner will tap twice. The Q4 options have captions on two of the four ("Growing strongly / Up meaningfully most years") and none on the other two. And there's a run of hyphens where the rest of the site uses em dashes ("Total sales - everything...", "last year's profit - they're paying", "a few key clients - especially"). Cosmetic, but a broker forwarding this to a client notices tidiness.
- On mobile: no horizontal scroll at 375px, tap targets measured 44–48px, base text 16px. Fine.

**Opportunity:** add a debt question and show a fourth number — "what you'd actually walk away with". That is the number the owner is really asking about, and no free tool gives it to them. It would also make the $999 look like a smaller fraction of something real.

## User Path — the price, and the login I couldn't get through

- Following "Start building your Business Genome" lands on **/plan**, where the price finally appears: **$999/month**, framed as "about 3.3% a year of what you stand to unlock". The trial terms are stated properly before the click — card saved today, nothing charged, first payment in 30 days, three days' warning by email, cancel any time. That's the right way round, and it's rare.
- **$999/month is a big number for the business your own example describes.** On my test case that's $11,988 a year against $260k of SDE — roughly 4.6% of everything the owner takes home. Framing it against the gap is clever, but the gap is a modelled figure and the $999 is a real direct debit. The owner-operators I act for think in terms of "what does it cost me a month against what I actually clear", and at $999 a lot of them stop reading.
- **Nowhere does it say whether that's plus GST.** This is an Australian product being sold to Australian businesses. "Is that inc or ex GST?" is the first question every single one of my clients asks, before "what does it do". Put it next to the number.
- The login page itself is correct: forgot-password link, a working eye toggle on the password field, a magic-link alternative, and a route through to sign-up. Going to /settings while signed out bounces cleanly to `/login?next=%2Fsettings`, so the gate holds and it remembers where I was headed.
- **I could not complete the sign-in, and I want to be precise about why so you don't chase a ghost.** My test browser hangs on this site's outbound requests — the sign-in request never resolves and the button sits on "Signing in…". I checked the same credentials directly against the auth service from the same machine and they authenticated in under half a second. So the fault is my end, not yours.
- **The consequence matters, though: I have not seen a single screen behind the login.** That includes the Settings "Plan & usage" panel — the second of the two things I told you were blocking me this morning. **I am not going to tell you it's fixed and I am not going to tell you it's still broken. I didn't see it.** Someone needs to log in as a genuinely brand-new account and read that panel with their own eyes before anyone signs this off.

**Opportunity:** show the price earlier. Right now an owner invests three minutes, gets emotionally invested in a $360k gap, and only then learns it's $999/month. That sequencing is deliberate and I understand it, but for the advisor channel it's backwards — I need to know the number before I introduce anyone, and the /advisors page currently tells brokers to go and run a valuation themselves to find it out.

## Advisor / Introducer Path

I read this as someone who'd be the introducer, and it's the most commercially serious page on the site.

- The terms are specific in a way that earns trust: 10% of what the owner pays, monthly, for the life of the subscription, on funds actually collected, with a monthly statement, paid to me or my firm. No joining fee, no minimum, no exclusivity.
- **First-touch attribution, stated as "it cannot be quietly reassigned".** That single sentence tells me somebody has been on the wrong end of a reassigned referral. It's the right model and the right way to say it.
- The visibility boundary is exactly right and stated three times consistently — on /advisors, in the Terms and in the Privacy Policy: I see that they signed up and how their valuation is moving; I never see their conversations. That's the boundary that makes this referable at all. If I could read my client's conversations with Kira, I couldn't introduce anyone.
- **The one condition needs an artefact, not just a promise.** "You only send it to owners you already hold a current listing or engagement agreement with, and you tell them you are paid a commission." Correct instinct. But for an accountant that disclosure sits under APES 110, and for a broker it sits under state agent legislation, and in both cases what's needed is a *record* that it was made. Give me a one-line disclosure I can paste into my own email, and record a tick against the introduction saying I confirmed I'd made it. Right now the obligation is mine and the evidence is nowhere.
- **GST on the 10% isn't addressed either.** If I invoice you for commission, am I invoicing $99 plus GST or $99 including? Small thing, blocks the first payment run.
- "Request access" is a form rather than a self-serve start, and I couldn't see the advisor dashboard behind the login, so I can't tell you whether "you watch it move" actually delivers anything useful.

**Opportunity:** publish an indicative price band on /advisors. Telling brokers "there's no price list, go run a valuation on a business you know" is charming once; the second time I'm asked to do homework before I can decide whether to refer, I close the tab.

## Other Strategic Feature Suggestions

1. **A one-page PDF the owner can hand to their accountant.** The results page has "Save / print this", but what gets referred is a document with the assumptions on it. Put the sector multiple, the SDE entered, the four uplift components and your BizBuySell provenance note on one page with the business name at the top, and every one of those PDFs that lands on an accountant's desk is a warm introduction to your advisor channel.
2. **Let owners re-run it and see movement.** The whole pitch is that the gap closes. A returning owner should see "your transferability score was 46 in July, it's 58 now" — that's the retention argument for $999/month, and it's the only thing that makes the number defensible at renewal.
3. **A succession variant of the questions.** Half my clients aren't selling to a stranger, they're handing to a son, a daughter or a manager. The valuation logic is identical but the framing ("what would they have to learn before you could step back?") is far more motivating than a sale price they don't want. It's the same product with a different set of question headings.
4. **Ask for the years trading and the lease.** Your own disclaimer says the number doesn't account for lease terms or how long you've been trading. Two more questions and it could.
5. **Give the advisor a co-branded link.** If I introduce a client, my logo should be on the results page they see. That's the difference between a tool I use and a tool I'm seen to use, and it's what makes an advisor actually send it.
