# Platform Feedback — Kira Walkthrough (fourth visit)

Hi Dennis,

**Persona:** Ray — 66, commercial electrical & mechanical contractor, 35 years in, Bunbury WA. Profitable, well known, and the whole thing runs on me. Thinking about selling in two to five years. Almost nobody knows.
**URL:** https://kira-rho.vercel.app
**Goal given:** Decide whether I'd actually pay for this and hand it to my broker.
**Duration:** ~55 minutes
**Date:** 2026-08-16

This is the fourth time I've walked through this. Last time I said I'd sign, on one condition — that the handover document got fixed. So I've spent this visit doing the thing properly: a real valuation on real numbers, my real pricing, my real people, and then I went and opened the document at the end of it.

I'll say up front what I found, because you'd rather know than be flattered: **the handover document is empty.** Not broken, not ugly — empty. I'll come to it.

---

## Landing page

- The headline does the job. *"You spent thirty years building it. Now sell it for what it's actually worth."* That's my situation in two lines, and the paragraph underneath about a buyer buying *you* rather than an asset is the argument my broker made to me, put better than he put it. I read the whole page before I touched anything and there was nothing on it that made me wince.
- The footer has the company name, the ABN and a street address. I check. Last thing I want is to hand my numbers to something with no name on it.
- I don't like the photograph. It's a young woman in a headset and it's plainly not a real person — the sort of picture that ends up on a call centre's brochure. Everything else on the page is written like a person talking to me and then there's a made-up face at the top of it. I'd sooner see the yard, a switchboard, or nothing at all.
- I clicked *"Talk to the assistant"* because the page says I can ask her who can see what I tell her, and that it will let me type if I'd rather. It went straight into a live microphone — Mute and End — with no typing box offered at any point, and then my browser tab died on me. I retried and it did the same. I can't tell you whether that's your end or mine, so I'm not calling it a bug, but I'll tell you what it *felt* like: I clicked the one thing on the page that promised I could type, it started listening to my office instead, and then it fell over. That's the moment a bloke like me shuts the laptop.
- **Opportunity:** put the typing box on screen *first*, with the microphone as the second choice. Everyone you're aiming at is sixty-plus and half of us are in an open office, a ute, or a house with a wife in the next room who doesn't know about any of this yet. Typing isn't the fallback for your customer. It's the default.

## The valuation questionnaire

- This is the best part of the product and it isn't close. Thirteen questions, plain English, and the explanation under each one is written like someone who has actually sat in a business. The SDE question in particular — *"if the business kept $120k after costs, paid you $50k in salary and perks, and paid $30k in interest, add all three and enter $200,000"* — is the first time anyone has explained add-backs to me in a way I didn't have to read twice. My own accountant doesn't do that.
- The last screen is the reason I kept going. Asking how long I want to keep running it, and then telling me *"this one answer stays on this device — it is not sent anywhere, not attached to any account, and it changes none of the figures"* — that's the single most reassuring sentence in the whole product. Somebody who has thought about my situation wrote that.
- Now the industry list, which I did not enjoy. **Gas Stations. Jewelry Stores. Gyms & Fitness Centers. Medical Billing. Day Care & Child Care. Routes (vending/distribution).** That's an American list with American spelling. We have service stations and jewellers and centres. "Medical Billing" isn't a business here. And there is nothing in it for mining services or civil, which in Western Australia is a large slice of who you're after — the closest I could get to what I do was "Electrical & Mechanical Contracting", which I'll accept, but I spent thirty seconds scrolling and thinking *this is an American tool with Australia pasted on the front*. You then tell me the sector sets my multiple. If the list is borrowed, the multiple is borrowed.
- Small one, but I read everything: on the opening screen there's a full stop with no space after it — *"the hardest thing to value.Answer 13 short questions"*.
- **Opportunity:** relabel the list for Australia and add the four or five sectors you're actually selling into — mining services, civil, transport, ag contracting. And say where the multiple came from *on the question screen*, not just in the small print at the end. "Sector sets your multiple, sourced from X" next to the dropdown would have stopped me worrying about it for the next ten minutes.

## The valuation result

- I'll be honest, I expected a sales page and got something closer to a piece of work. Three numbers, then what's left after the $310k the business owes and the $340k owed to me, then a straight warning that the yard sitting in my super fund isn't in any of it and that a buyer will re-work my profit at market rent. Nobody has told me that before. That paragraph alone is worth the three minutes.
- The audit trail at the bottom — every answer I gave, listed back, with the source of the multiples named and the date on it — is the thing that made me take the rest seriously. I could print that and put it in front of my accountant tomorrow.
- **But the arithmetic flatters me, and that bothers me more than a low number would.** I told it my business would *struggle* without me, that my processes are *mostly in my head*, and it scored me 30 out of 100 for transferability. The page then says half the weighting is whether a buyer can take it over at all. And my multiple came out at **2.7× against a sector median of 2.9× — a seven per cent discount.** Seven per cent, for a business that is admittedly 70% me. Then the captured figure is 3.2×, *above* the sector median, on the strength of writing things down. My broker knocked a third off in his head and told me why. Your calculator knocked off seven per cent. One of you is wrong and I know which one I'd bet on, and it isn't the one telling me what I want to hear.
- The same thing shows in the gap. Your own plumber example on the landing page goes from $626k to $821k — a lift of about a third. Mine goes from $1.70m to $1.97m, a lift of sixteen per cent, and I'm a worse case than the plumber on nearly every answer. The example on the front page promises roughly twice the movement of the calculator behind it.
- **Opportunity:** widen the owner-dependence penalty until a 30/100 business is priced like a 30/100 business, and let the honest number be low. I came here half-resenting a broker for telling me a hard truth. A tool that tells me a softer one doesn't win me over — it tells me it's selling something. The whole page is built on being straight with me; the multiplier is the one place it isn't.

## $270,000 or $271,000

- The valuation result says the value locked in my head is **$270,000**. I click through to the plan page and the headline says **$270,000** and then, four inches further down the *same page*, a purple panel says **"You could unlock $271,000"**. I then find the figure a third time inside my Genome, where the entry reads *"so the gap is $271,000"* while the header of that same page says $270,000.
- It's a thousand dollars and it doesn't change anything. That's not the point. The point is I am being asked to pay real money on the strength of a calculation, and the calculation can't hold one number still across two panels of one screen. That is exactly what I would look for if I were trying to decide whether to believe you, and I found it in the first minute of looking.
- **Opportunity:** round once, at the source, and print the same figure everywhere. It's a five-minute fix that removes the only reason I had to distrust the maths.

## The plan and pricing page

- The billing terms are the clearest I've seen anywhere. Billed in arrears, the month you're in is never charged, three days' warning before every payment, cancel in one click, a hard ceiling at twelve months whether or not you think the work is done, GST stated on every figure. There is no trick in it and it reads like there's no trick in it. Whoever wrote that understood that my generation reads terms.
- $999 + GST a month against a $270k gap is about twelve grand a year to unlock a quarter of a million. On the arithmetic I don't argue with it.
- **The invitation code I was given did not work.** I opened *Enter your invitation code*, typed it in, and got *"That code is not valid. Check it against the email we sent you — or reply to it and we will send a new one."* There is no email. I don't have one to reply to. If that code was already used on one of my earlier visits then the message is also wrong — "not valid" and "already redeemed" are different problems with different fixes, and the message sends me chasing an email that doesn't exist. For an invited beta user that's a full stop with nowhere to go.
- **There is no Sign in link anywhere on this page.** I already have an account from last time. The page I was sent to has a logo, a "Redo my valuation" link, and a button to start paying. To get to my own account I had to go back to the landing page and find it in the top corner. I wouldn't create a second account, so what I'd actually have done is given up.
- **Opportunity:** put "Already have an account? Sign in" under the Start now button, and make the invitation error distinguish "we don't recognise this" from "this one's been used". Both are one line of work and both are the difference between a customer and a closed tab.

## Setting her up

- I signed in and landed on **"Kira isn't set up yet."** I've been here before. Whatever I did on those visits is gone and I'm starting from nothing. I appreciate this is a test account and you may have cleared it, but from where I sat it read as *nothing you told us survived*, which for a product whose entire pitch is "she remembers" is a bad first sentence.
- Then something genuinely good: it found the valuation I'd just run on this computer and asked **"That's mine — use it"** or **"Not mine — discard it"**, and explained why — *"if someone else used this computer, discard it."* That is precisely the right instinct for my situation and I've not seen another product do it.
- The review screen — *"Check we have this right"* — puts my brief in a box three lines tall and cuts it off mid-sentence. I'm being asked to check something I can't see without scrolling inside a small window. Make the box tall enough to hold what's in it; this is the one screen where reading matters.
- The button says **"Review Framework"**. I don't know what a Framework is and I'm not going to guess. Same with the sidebar, which calls it **Overview**, while the page I land on calls itself **your dashboard**. Two names for one place, and one of them is a word I'd never use.
- **Opportunity:** on the setup screen, ask me the business's name and what it does. You'll see in a minute why that one missing question causes real damage further down.

## Talking to her — the part I came for

I gave her two real things, the way I would give them to a person.

**First, how I price.** $118 an hour on service plus materials at cost plus 22%. Tendered work at 18% over labour and materials, except mine sites at 28% because of the standdowns and the inductions. Long-standing builders on a straightforward job, I'll drop to 12. None of it written down; I do it in my head looking at the drawings.

She read it straight back, accurately, every number right, and asked if I'd like it captured in a document. Good.

**Second, my people.** Gary, leading hand, 24 years with me, the only other person who can price a job — and he's 61. Sharon does invoicing, payroll and the ATO, three days, and she's my sister-in-law. Dylan's a fourth-year and he's the one I'd want to keep. Only Gary knows the mine-site loading. Nobody at all knows I'm thinking of selling.

She read that back accurately too, and finished with: **"Ready for what's next or anything to adjust?"**

- That is the whole problem in one line. I have just told her that the second of two people who can price work is 61, that one commercial edge exists only in his head, and that I'm planning an exit nobody knows about. A buyer's advisor would have put his pen down. He'd have said *Ray, Gary is the number. If Gary goes, half your ability to win work goes with him, and he's four years from a pension. What are we doing about that before we do anything else?* She said "ready for what's next".
- **She never mentioned Gary's age.** She wrote it down — it's in the record, "age 61" — and drew nothing from it. That's the difference between a filing cabinet and an advisor, and the front page sells me the advisor: *"asks the questions you haven't thought of"*, *"pushes back when something is unclear"*. She did neither, twice.
- So I asked her outright: if you were the buyer's advisor across the table, what would worry you most? **And she was good.** Key-person concentration on me and Gary, pricing undocumented, critical knowledge held by too few people, and a sensible three-month plan. Still nothing about Gary's age — but a decent answer.
- Which tells you the judgement is in there. She just waits to be asked. And the customer you've described on your own front page — the bloke who has told nobody, who doesn't know what he doesn't know — is *precisely* the customer who will never think to ask. If she only performs when prompted, she's a search box with manners, and I can get that free.
- **Opportunity:** make her react before she files. One sentence, after the read-back, naming the risk in what I just said: *"Ray — before we go on. Gary is 61 and he's your only other pricer. That's the biggest single thing holding your number down. Can we work on that first?"* That one sentence is the entire difference between $999 a month and a note-taker, and it would have made me sit up. She has already proved she can write it. She just needs to volunteer it.

## "Where is the document?"

- She'd offered to write up my pricing. I said yes please. Nothing appeared, so I asked her plainly: *you said you'd put the pricing into a document for me to review — where is it?*
- Her answer: *"I've drafted a document summarising your pricing model… **It's ready to send as a formal note. What email address should I use for the recipient?**"*
- I asked to **read** it. She offered to **send** it, and asked me for somebody's email address. I've told her in the same conversation that nobody knows I'm selling. That is the one direction I am frightened of, and it's her first instinct.
- It's also something she can't do — the top of my own dashboard says in a yellow banner that she can't send email as me yet. So she offered me the one thing she can't do instead of the one thing I asked for.
- And there is nowhere in the app to read a draft. The dashboard lists *"Pricing model summary for service and tendered work — drafted and waiting on your go-ahead"* with a button that says "Talk to Kira about these", which takes me back to the chat, which offers to email it. The Knowledge page is empty. The document exists somewhere and I cannot look at it.
- **Opportunity:** every draft needs a title you can click that opens the text, on a page, with an Approve button under it. Nothing should be sendable before it's readable. And "send" should never be the answer to "where is it".

## My Genome

- The page itself is good work. Nine areas named as the questions a buyer's advisor actually asks — *"Could someone else price a job and reach your number?"*, *"Who would leave on announcement?"* — is exactly the right framing, and the four-step fill indicator (*you mentioned it → she has the basics → most of it is written down → a buyer could use it*) is honest about the difference between telling and capturing.
- **The privacy marking works and it's the best thing in the product.** My staff note came back tagged *"Yours only — kept out of the handover document, because it touches on who you have and have not told."* It picked up, on its own, that "nobody knows I'm thinking of selling" must never leave the building. That is the single thing that would make me trust this with the rest.
- **Bug: the same fact is stored twice.** There is an entry called *"Pricing model"* and another called *"Pricing approach"* and they are my pricing, word for word the same content, written out twice. I told her once. If she doubles everything I say, then in six months my "operating manual" is two of everything and I'm the one who has to work out which one is current.
- **After both disclosures, the page says "0 of 9 started, 0 well covered."** Transferability still 30 out of 100. The area called *"Who does the work"* — the one my staff conversation answers directly — says **"Nothing yet"** and *"Kira has not been shown where this lives."* I've just told her who does the work. Five things captured, none of them filed.
- I understand there's a lag and the page says so. But the number on the front of this product is the number I'm buying — *watch it go up* — and I gave it two substantial pieces of my business and watched nothing move at all. If that's a filing delay, then the delay is the most demoralising thing in the product, and it lands on day one.
- **Opportunity:** file it against the area the moment she captures it, even provisionally, and let me confirm or move it. An unfiled shelf that never empties is worse than a wrong guess I can correct — and as I'm about to explain, unfiled doesn't just look bad, it costs me the whole deliverable.

## The handover document — the thing I said had to be fixed

I downloaded it. It opens in a browser with no login, it prints, it's mine to keep, and the mechanics of that are exactly as promised. Then I read it.

- **It contains nothing.** The header says *"0 entries across 0 of 9 areas"*. All nine sections say the same sentence: *"Nothing recorded here yet. This is still carried by the owner alone."* My pricing isn't in it. My people aren't in it. Nothing I said today is in it.
- The reason is the filing. Everything I told her is sitting in the unfiled pile, and the unfiled pile doesn't go into the handover copy. So the document only ever contains facts that have completed a step that, on my account, nothing has completed. My own copy has the five entries — under a heading called *unfiled* — and the broker's copy has none.
- **And the page told me otherwise.** It says the handover document *"covers the business and leaves out anything marked yours only above."* My pricing was **not** marked yours-only. By that sentence it should be in there. It isn't. The page is describing a rule the file isn't following.
- **The document is titled "This business".** That's the heading. Not my company's name — she never asked me for it, not once, in setup or in conversation. So the thing I would hand a broker is a document called *This business* about an unnamed company containing nine empty sections. I would not send that to a man I want to take me seriously. I'd be embarrassed.
- Then I opened **Share**. It writes the email for me: *"I have put together a record of how the business actually runs — where the work comes from, how it is priced, who does what, and the parts that still depend on me."* Underneath that it attaches the empty document. It is a note in my name making a claim about a file that contains none of it. And it signs off **"dennis+ray"** — the front half of my email address, as if that were my name.
- To be fair, there's a *"Read what they will get"* link before you send, and I'd probably have used it. But the send button is right there next to it, the covering note is already written and confident, and there is no warning anywhere that the document has nothing in it. The one action in this product that reaches another human being is the one with the least protection on it.
- **Opportunity:** three things, in this order. (1) Unfiled facts must appear in the handover document — put them under a plain heading like "Other things the owner has told us" rather than dropping them. An untidy document that says something beats a tidy one that says nothing. (2) Refuse to share a document with zero entries, and say why. (3) Ask me the business's name in setup and put it at the top of the page, and use my first name in the signature, not my email prefix.

## Other strategic feature suggestions

- **You are three different websites.** The landing page is white and dark green with a serif. The valuation is cream and yellow with a pink button and a purple brain. The app is grey and white with a purple button and an orange wordmark. I went from one to the next to the next inside five minutes and twice checked I hadn't been redirected somewhere. For a product whose entire job is to make me feel safe handing over my life's work, looking like three companies is not a cosmetic problem.
- **The valuation should be able to run twice.** The most useful thing you could give me isn't the number, it's *"here is your number if you fix the Gary problem and nothing else."* You already compute the four buckets. Let me tick one and watch the figure move. That's what would make me pay, and it's the demonstration that closes the sale — right now I take the $270k entirely on faith.
- **Print or email me the valuation.** There's a Print button and no way to have it sent to me. I ran it on the office computer. I'd want to read it again at home at nine at night when there's nobody about, and the only copy of it is on the machine my bookkeeper uses.
- **The "Report a problem" tab sits on top of the buttons.** On my phone it lands directly over the *"Talk to Kira about these"* button on the dashboard. On the laptop it hovers over the middle of the valuation page. It's your feedback widget covering the thing you want people to press.
- **Tell me who else has seen it.** The copy says your support team can see what's captured "when they need to keep the service running". I accept that, but I'd want a page that lists it — who looked, and when. For a man who hasn't told his wife yet, "someone might look" without "here's when they did" is the sort of thing I'd lie awake on.
- **The dashboard opens on a limitation.** The first thing on my home screen is a yellow box explaining that she can't send email as me. I didn't come here to send email. I came to get what's in my head onto paper. Lead with the work, not the plumbing.

## Standards check (portfolio non-negotiables)

**Failures first — these are findings, not nitpicks.**

- ❌ **Zero dead ends** — the invitation code fails with an error pointing me at an email I never got; `/plan` has no Sign in link for a returning owner; a "drafted" document exists with no screen anywhere that displays it.
- ❌ **Consistent visual identity** — three distinct design languages across marketing (white/green/serif), valuation (cream/pink/purple) and app (grey/purple/orange).
- ❌ **Consequence clarity on the Share action** — the covering note asserts a record of the business exists; the attached document has zero entries; nothing warns before send. (Delete Account and the billing terms are handled properly — this one specific action is not.)
- ❌ **Touch targets ≥44px / no collisions** — the "Report a problem" pill overlays the primary CTA on the mobile dashboard and hovers over content on desktop.
- ⚠️ **Voice agent** — reachable from the chrome in two clicks (Overview → Talk to Kira → Talk to the assistant), so placement passes. But clicking it on the landing page put me straight into a live microphone with no typing option offered, and the tab then died twice. I can't separate your end from mine, so I'm marking this **not verified**, not failed.

**Passes.**

- ✅ **Responsive** — checked at 375px and 1440px on landing, valuation, result, dashboard and Genome. No horizontal scroll at either width; the app nav collapses to a hamburger; text is readable without zooming.
- ✅ **Auth-page pattern** — forgot-password link, show-password toggle and "Email me a magic link" all present on `/login`. Password sign-in worked first time.
- ✅ **Authenticated chrome + Settings** — persistent left nav on every signed-in page (Overview / My Genome / Knowledge), with Settings and Sign out anchored at the bottom and the account email shown. `/settings` carries Profile, Business details, Connected accounts, Plan & usage, Password, Notifications, Signed-in devices and Delete account, each with an explanation.
- ✅ **Explanatory headers** — every page opens by saying what it is and what to do, and the empty states keep it (Knowledge and the un-started Genome areas both explain themselves).
- ✅ **Browser tab title** — "Kira — your part-time general manager".
- ✅ **Legal surfaces** — Privacy and Terms both live and reachable from the footer; company name, ABN and postal address printed.
- ✅ **Prices carry tax** — every figure I saw was "+ GST", including inside the FAQ and on the plan card.
- — **Team admin** — not applicable; this is a single-owner product and correctly has no organisation layer.
- — **Address autocomplete** — the only location field is "City, Country", which is a fair use of free text. No street-address field encountered.

## Would I sign?

**Not this time. And I want to be precise about why, because it isn't the same reason as before.**

Everything I asked for on the visit before this has been built. The document downloads, it opens without an account, it prints, it's mine if I stop paying. The billing terms are honest. The privacy marking works and is better than I expected — she worked out on her own that "nobody knows I'm selling" must never leave the building, and that is not a small thing.

But the file is **empty**, and it's empty for a structural reason rather than a cosmetic one: what I tell her lands in an unfiled pile, and the unfiled pile never reaches the handover copy. So the product's only deliverable is guaranteed to be blank on day one, in week one, and for however long it takes that pile to clear — while the Genome page shows me 0 of 9 and a transferability score that hasn't moved a point since before I signed up. I paid to watch a number go up. Both numbers stood still.

And underneath that is the thing that will decide whether I renew, not whether I join. I gave her the two hardest facts about my business — a 61-year-old who is half my pricing capability, and an exit nobody knows about — and she filed them and asked what was next. She *can* do better; when I asked her straight out she gave me a proper answer. But your customer doesn't know the question. If she only performs on request, she is a very well-mannered filing cabinet, and I've got one of those in the corner already.

**Three things and I'd sign, today.**

1. **Unfiled facts go in the handover document.** Under a rough heading if you must. A messy document that has my pricing in it is worth something to a broker; a tidy one with nine empty sections is worth less than nothing, because it tells him I've got nothing.
2. **She reacts before she files.** One sentence naming the risk in what I just said, unprompted, every time. Gary being 61 should have stopped the conversation.
3. **Hold one number still.** $270,000 or $271,000, pick one, and print it everywhere. And while you're in there, decide whether a 30-out-of-100 business really only takes a seven per cent haircut — because right now the calculator is kinder to me than my broker was, and I noticed.

Fix one and two and I'll put my card in. Fix all three and I'll ring two blokes I know who are in the same boat and haven't told anyone either.
