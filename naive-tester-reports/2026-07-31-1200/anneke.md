## Landing page

- The hero primary button — "Find out in 3 minutes →" — is not under the hero copy. At 1440px it sits **out in the left gutter**, floating beside the story carousel, vertically centred against nothing. It reads as a stray sticky element, not as the main call to action. The secondary link ("Or watch what happens over six months") is properly centred above it, which makes the misplacement more obvious, not less. Screenshot `03-hero-scroll700.png`.
- The three headline numbers on the hero card — WALK AWAY $220k / TODAY $582k / CAPTURED $1.02M — carry no definition. On the calculator's own intro screen you define all three beautifully ("the walk-away value — if you just sold the gear and closed the doors"). On the hero, where 90% of visitors will see them, they're three bare figures. "Walk away" in my trade usually means *what the vendor walks away with after the sale* — net proceeds. Here it means liquidation. Two opposite readings of the same phrase, and the wrong one flatters you.
- The testimonials are attributed to "Business". Four of them, all attributed to the word "Business" with an emoji. I understand why — no real customers yet, and you have been scrupulously honest about beta status everywhere else. But an unattributed quote is worse than no quote, because I have spent twenty-five years watching people manufacture those. "Plumbing business, 9 staff, Brisbane — name withheld until they've told their staff" would be *more* credible, not less, and it fits your own thesis about owners not having told anyone yet.
- Terminology: the tab title and the hero say "your fractional exec", but the entire page argues you are a *sale-readiness* product. "Fractional exec" is a phrase my clients have never heard. Not one owner-operator in his sixties has ever used it to me.
- Opportunity: put the sample Genome page one click from the hero, not behind "What you get" in the nav. The Genome is your product. The hero currently sells a feeling; the Genome sells the thing.

## The free valuation

This is the strongest funnel piece on the site and I'd genuinely use it in a first meeting. The question set is better than most paid tools: you ask about **client concentration** with proper bands, **owner absence** ("if you took a 3-month holiday tomorrow"), **locked-in revenue**, and you explain SDE add-backs correctly, with a worked example. That's a professional question set, not a lead-magnet quiz.

Then it lands, and here's what I'd have to walk back with a client.

- **The walk-away number contradicts its own caption.** I entered $250,000 of gear/vehicles/stock. The result reads: **WALK AWAY $250,000**, and directly beneath it, *"Book value of the gear — a quick auction typically returns 40–60c in the dollar."* So the product tells the owner his floor is $250k and then tells him it's really $100–150k, in the same box. Pick one. If you believe the 40–60c line — and you should, it's right — then the headline figure is the auction range, and the book value is the caption. Right now the number an owner clings to as his worst case is overstated by about double, by your own footnote. Screenshot `09-valuation-result-top.png`.
- **The captured multiple is above the Australian market.** My test: electrical contractor, $3.2M turnover, $520k SDE, growing steadily, top client 10–30%, owner heavily involved, processes partly documented, some recurring. Result: today $1,693,153 at ~3.3× SDE; captured $2,651,087 at **~5.1× SDE**. The 3.3× I would defend in a room. The 5.1× I would not. For a trade services business of that size in this market I am realistically arguing 2.5–3.5× SDE, and 4× is a very good day with a genuine second-in-charge and a forward order book. If my client walks in holding a screen that says $2.65M and I have to tell him $1.9M, the damage lands on me, not on you.
- To your enormous credit, the footnote says exactly this: BizBuySell 2025 US data, ~9,500 deals, market average ~2.5× SDE, no equivalent AU dataset, *"the absolute number should be checked against local evidence before anyone acts on it."* That is the most honest methodology note I have ever seen on a valuation tool. It is also in grey 13px at the bottom of the page, underneath four boxes that say "+$404,506 once captured". Nobody in their sixties reads to the bottom of that page. **Move the "indicative, US-benchmarked, check locally" line directly under the three headline figures**, where the number is.
- **False precision.** $1,693,153. $957,934. +$201,247. Six significant figures on a model whose inputs include the words "Holding steady" and "A few big ones". No valuer I know would ever quote to the dollar, and an owner who has been quoted to the dollar assumes you *know*. Round everything to two figures — $1.7M, $2.65M, ~$950k. That single change buys you more credibility than any disclaimer.
- The internal arithmetic is sound and I checked it: the four "where the value is hiding" amounts sum to the gap exactly. The "why the number is what it is" explanation (buyers price *risk*, the car-service-history analogy) is the clearest version of that argument I've read. Keep it.
- **"Clear my answers" wipes everything, from question 1 through 10, with no confirmation and no undo.** It sits three inches from "Next", in the same card, all the way through. An owner doing this on a phone will hit it. Confirm before you clear — this is exactly the kind of thing my clients ring me about.
- Terminology: on the growth question, "Growing strongly" and "Flat" carry sub-captions, "Growing steadily" and "Declining" don't. Reads as unfinished.
- Save-and-resume works — I closed and reloaded mid-flow and it put me back at question 4. Good, and the "7 days, on this device only, nothing is sent anywhere" line under the start button is the right disclosure at the right moment.
- Missing fields a buyer's accountant will ask for on day one, none of which are in the eleven: **the premises lease and its option terms**, and **whether the trade licence sits with the entity or with the owner personally**. A 66-year-old electrician whose licence is in his own name is the single most common reason a business sale becomes an asset sale at a worse multiple, and it isn't asked anywhere.
- Opportunity: add a twelfth question — "does your trade licence sit with the business or with you personally?" — and let a wrong answer visibly move the number. That one screen would justify the whole product to every broker in the country.

## Pricing and the plan page

- The price only appears after the valuation, at `/plan`. It **is** genuinely gap-banded, and I checked: my $958k-gap electrician saw **Growth plan, $999/month + GST**; a hairdressing salon with a $185k gap saw **Starter plan, $499/month + GST**. So the "her fee is set to the size of your gap" claim is true, not marketing. Good, and the "+ GST" is present on every instance — no ambiguity about what leaves the account.
- But look at what the two bands actually mean. The big business pays 1.3% a year of its upside; the salon pays **3.2%** — two and a half times the proportion, for the smaller business with less money. The copy ("a small fraction of what you stand to unlock") implies proportionality that the bands don't deliver. If the bands are going to stay coarse, don't lean so hard on the percentage framing.
- The circularity will be noticed. The thing that justifies $999/month is a $958k gap calculated by the vendor selling the $999/month, using a US multiple set. Every accountant my clients use will say that out loud. The defence is easy and you already have it — the honest methodology footnote — but it needs to be *near the price*, not two pages back.
- The beta disclosure is exemplary: "Stripe is in test mode, no card is charged, no real subscription is created, we will email you before billing is switched on." I would repeat that to a client without checking it.
- Opportunity: a "what this costs against what you'd otherwise pay" line. My brokerage on a $1.7M sale is $70–85k, once. Two years of Kira at $999 is roughly $26k. That comparison makes the price look modest and it's true; right now the only comparison offered is against the product's own uplift estimate, which is the weakest available.

I did not complete a submission, so I can't vouch that a lead actually arrives — worth someone confirming end to end before the first real broker uses it.
- Opportunity: give me a one-page client-facing leave-behind — the three numbers, the Genome sample, the disclaimer — that I can hand across a kitchen table. Right now the only shareable thing is a URL, and the link preview (see below) is not something I'd send.

## Voice — the part that worries me most

The whole product is "just talk to her". So the mic is not a feature, it's the entire surface.

I clicked "Talk to the assistant" in a browser with no microphone — which in the real world is a client who tapped "Don't Allow", or an iPad with Safari locked down, or a work laptop with device permissions managed. Screenshot `22-voice-attempt.png`. What appeared:

- **Mute** and **End** buttons, as though a call were running, and
- the words **"Not supported"** in red.

Nothing else. No explanation, no "here's how to allow the microphone", no support link, and — I checked under "More", which offers only Add knowledge / Share Kira / Complete project — **no text fallback of any kind**. There is no way to use this product without a working microphone.

So the failure mode for my client is: he pays, he taps the one button, he gets a red "Not supported" next to a Mute button for a call that isn't happening, and he rings me. That's the call I don't want.

- Also in that "More" menu: **"Share Kira"**, with no statement of what gets shared or what the recipient can see. For a product whose founding premise is *"most owners start this before they've told anyone"*, a share control with no consequence copy is the wrong default. Say plainly what a recipient can and cannot see, and confirm before it happens.
- Opportunity: a typed fallback. Even a plain text box that routes to the same assistant would turn total product failure into a mild inconvenience, and it would open the product to every owner whose hearing isn't what it was — which, in my client base, is a lot of them.

## The Genome — the actual product

The public example at `/genome` is the best thing you have built and it is what would make me pick up the phone.

*"Three builders supply roughly 60% of turnover. Hartley Constructions, Vaughan Homes and Ridge Developments. All three came through the owner personally; Hartley since 1998. None are on a written contract — work is allocated by a phone call to the owner, usually on a Friday."*

That is the paragraph I spend six weeks extracting from a vendor, and it is stated plainly instead of buried. The **"You confirmed this"** versus **"Captured — not yet confirmed"** distinction is what makes it usable in due diligence rather than just interesting. And the **"Still only in your head"** section — naming the gaps instead of glossing them — is the move that separates this from every "business health check" I've been pitched.

The real product at `/my-genome` delivers on it too: 44 items, "41 of them are dated to the conversation you said them in — that is what a buyer's accountant will want to see", and both exports work. I pulled the handover document and it does carry the "indicative figures from a self-reported valuation, not a formal appraisal" line, exactly as the advisor page promises. That claim is real. Good.

Now the gaps:

- **The handover document is a `.md` file.** `business-genome-2026-07-31.md`, markdown, downloaded as an attachment. This is the deliverable the entire product is sold on — *"one pack a buyer's accountant or solicitor can read cold"* — and it is in a format that a 60-year-old accountant cannot open, cannot print sensibly and would not accept. It needs to be a PDF with page numbers.
- **It's titled to the person, not the business**: "# Business Genome — Dennis McMahon". A handover pack must be headed with the trading name and the entity — "Factory2Key, The Trustee for Factory2Key Unit Trust, ABN 51 700 805 298". You already hold all of that in Settings; it just isn't on the document.
- The example Genome dates entries **"Conversation, 12 March"** — no year. In a pack read cold in a data room, an entry without a year is worthless.
- The example only expands its first section. The other five show a percentage and a question. Show me two expanded, so I can see the depth range.
- **The single most important artifact is described but never shown.** "A handover document — one pack a buyer's accountant or solicitor can read cold. This is the thing that shortens due diligence." Two sentences, no sample. Show me one page of it. That page is the whole sale.
- Categorisation is going wrong in the live product. Under **"How work comes in — where does revenue come from, and does it depend on you?"** the top entry was *"An in-app phone call system will be built using mobile contacts…"* — a software project, filed under revenue sources. In a document a buyer reads cold, a misfiled entry under "revenue" is worse than a missing one.
- Sections a broker needs that aren't there: **staff** (the example says 9 staff and never mentions who does what, who is key, who has a restraint of trade), **the premises lease and its options**, and **plant/vehicle ownership versus finance**. "Licences, insurance and the calendar" gestures at part of it. These three are where deals die.
- US spelling throughout the captured entries — "prioritizes", "organizing", "analyzing" — on an Australian product going into an Australian data room.

## `/what-she-does` — and one hole in it

*"Talk you through your profit and loss: she can reach the report and currently reads only the income line out of it, which is half an answer and worse than none. Until she can give you the whole picture she will not pretend to."*

I have never seen a vendor write that sentence. It is worth more to me than any feature list.

Which is exactly why the hole matters. That page states: **"Xero is the only system she connects to, and only to read it."**

- I could find **no way to connect Xero anywhere in the product.** Settings → Connected accounts offers Google only. Yet the page describes "Who owes me?", "What do I owe?" and account balances "read out of Xero once you have connected it" as things she *does*. On the one page whose entire purpose is separating what she does from what she doesn't, describing an integration a customer cannot reach is precisely the failure it exists to prevent.
- Conversely, Settings shows **"Google — Drive: read and write"** plus Contacts access. **Write** access to a client's Google Drive is a material disclosure, and it appears nowhere on the page that lists what she connects to. If I am introducing a client, I need that on the page, in that plain language, not discovered in Settings after signup.

## Other Strategic Feature Suggestions

- **Give me a "walk-back range", not a point estimate.** Show today and captured as bands ($1.6M–$1.9M) rather than dollars. It's more honest, it matches how any valuer speaks, and it removes the moment where I have to correct your number in front of my client.
- **A licence/lease/staff readiness flag on the result screen.** Three yes/no questions that visibly move the number would make the tool indispensable to every broker in the country, because those three are what I chase for six weeks.
- **Ship the handover pack as a PDF and show a sample page publicly.** That artifact is the product. Right now it is the least visible thing on the site and the hardest to open.
- **A quarterly "readiness movement" email to the introducing advisor.** You already commit to showing me progress and never contents. One email a quarter — "your client's transferability moved 41 → 58" — is my reason to come back, and my reason to introduce the next one.
- **Let me white-label the introduction.** Not the whole product — just a landing page with my firm's name on it saying "Anneke has asked us to help you get this out of your head." My clients trust me, not a brand they've never heard of. This is a much bigger lever on your channel than the commission rate.
- **Reconsider the "current listing or engagement agreement" condition.** Replace it with "owners you have a genuine professional relationship with, not purchased lists." As written it locks out the pre-listing owner your product is built for, which is most of my pipeline.
- **Say something about death and incapacity.** Your advisor FAQ answers "if my client sells, retires or dies, does their information come out?" beautifully. Owners in their sixties think about this more than you'd guess, and it's a genuine reason to start today rather than next year. It deserves a line on the owner-facing site, not only on mine.

## VERDICT: **FAIL** for the share gate.

Four things block it, and none of them are opinions:

1. **The walk-away number contradicts its own caption on the results screen.** The headline overstates an owner's floor by roughly double, per your own footnote directly beneath it. That is a wrong number, in the largest type on the page, about the thing my client cares most about.
2. **The voice failure state is a dead end with no fallback.** No microphone means no product, and the screen says "Not supported" beside a Mute button for a call that isn't happening.
3. **Another person's first name is displayed on the account dashboard**, on a product whose headline promise to advisors is that nothing crosses between clients. I could not establish any real access leak and I am not alleging one — but it is visible, and visible is enough to end a referral conversation.
4. **The `/advisors` page has no mobile navigation at all** — the one surface built for the channel, on the device the channel reads email on.

Add to those the raw `Kira_Dennis_FigureOut_7f1c` identifiers and the third-person truncated "The user reiterated the need for a streamlined onboarding wizard" as the first line of the paid product. None of those four are hard fixes; all four are things a client would hit in his first ten minutes.

**Would I refer a client today? No.** But I want to be precise about why, because it is not the usual reason.

The usual reason is that the product is thin and the promises are inflated. That is not the case here. The promises are, if anything, *under*-claimed — you tell me what isn't built before I ask, you publish the methodology's weakness in your own footnote, and `/what-she-does` volunteers that she reads only the income line off a P&L and will not pretend otherwise. I have never had a vendor do that. The `/genome` example is the artifact I have wanted for fifteen years.

The reason is that the finish doesn't match the thinking. My clients are sixty-six, they are not confident with software, they are doing something they have not told their wife about yet, and they will read a red "Not supported", a truncated machine sentence, and a stranger's name on their own screen as evidence that this is not safe to trust with the most sensitive thing they own. They will not distinguish a display bug from a data breach. They will just stop.

Fix those four, put the handover pack out as a PDF, round the numbers, and move the honest methodology line up next to the figures it qualifies — and I will send you the first three clients myself, beta payments switched off and all. The commission is not why I would do it. The Genome is.
