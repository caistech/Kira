# Ray — second walkthrough of Kira, 16 August 2026

Hi Dennis,

You asked me back to look at the same thing again. Last time I told you the nine areas
meant nothing to me and that the panels behind them were a room with no door at all.

Short version: **the door is fitted, and on a brand-new account it is locked.** I found
that out the hard way, and I'll take you through it. But I want to start somewhere else,
because the first twenty minutes were the best twenty minutes I have spent with a piece
of software in years.

---

## 1. The valuation — this is the product, and you should know it

Thirteen questions, three minutes, no sign-up, and the number came back with more
honesty in it than the broker gave me over an hour and a half.

What got me was not the figure. It was the paragraphs around it:

- "The gear figure is a range because auction recovery genuinely is one — 40 to 60 cents
  in the dollar is well documented. The two business figures are single numbers because
  our sector data gives one median multiple and no spread around it; putting a ± on them
  would be inventing a precision we do not have."
- The bit about the yard. I own it through the super fund, and you told me — before
  anybody else has — that a buyer will re-work my profit at market rent and that some of
  what I think is profit is really rent I have chosen not to take. Nobody has said that
  to me. My accountant hasn't said that to me.
- "You told us this on the last question. It stayed on this device — it is not attached
  to any account, it was not sent anywhere, and it changed none of the figures above."
  I answered *within two years* to that question and I have not told my wife that. The
  fact you told me, unprompted, where that answer went is the reason I kept clicking.

The four "where the value is hiding" lines added up exactly to the $210,000 headline. I
checked. That matters more than you'd think — a number that doesn't reconcile is how I
know I'm being sold to.

Two nits. You say "Answer 13 short questions" at the top and "what the eleven questions
decide" in the footnotes. Pick one; I count things for a living. And the last screen
asks three questions after telling me there were thirteen, which made me wonder whether
I'd been counted properly.

**Opportunity:** this page is worth more than the subscription behind it. If a broker
could send it to a client under his own name and get the reply, you would not need to
sell anything else. I'd have paid a few hundred dollars for the valuation alone and
walked away happy, which is a compliment and also a warning about what comes next.

## 2. The nine areas — yes, I can read the picture now

You redrew it. Here's exactly what I see.

Nine tiles in a three-by-three grid. Each has a little stack of four boxes above the
name, filling from the bottom. Six of mine have the bottom box coloured orange with a
tag saying "You told us". Two are all grey saying "Nothing yet". After I talked to her,
one turned green with two boxes filled, saying "Just started".

So — can I tell at a glance how far along each area is? **Roughly, yes.** Orange versus
grey is instantly legible. That is a real improvement and you should keep it.

But three things stop it short:

**There is no key.** Four boxes. One is filled. What are the other three? Nothing on the
page tells me what the second, third and fourth boxes mean, or what I would have to do
to fill them. I'm looking at a progress bar for a journey nobody has described. If the
four steps are something like *nothing / you mentioned it / she has it written down /
a buyer could use it* — say so, once, under the grid. Right now I'm guessing, and a man
guessing about his own business is not the feeling you're selling.

**Green is doing the wrong job.** Orange = "You told us" (one box). Green = "Just
started" (two boxes). So the *further along* state is green and the *barely there* state
is orange. My eye reads green as done and orange as needs-attention, which is exactly
backwards from what you mean. Either use one colour and let the fill do the talking, or
make green mean genuinely covered and nothing else.

**"0 of 9 well covered so far"** sits directly above six tiles with coloured bars in
them. See section 3.

**Opportunity:** put a one-line legend under the grid — four words per level. It costs
you nothing and it converts a decoration into an instrument.

## 3. Does the page agree with itself? No, and the numbers are worse than the words

On one screen, at one moment, I was told:

| Where | What it said |
|---|---|
| Above the grid | "0 of 9 well covered so far" |
| Six of the nine tiles | "You told us", with a coloured bar |
| Below the grid | "Nothing is filled in yet" |
| Big dotted panel below that | "**Nothing captured yet**" |
| After I talked to her | "**7 things captured**, across 1 of the 9 areas" |
| The same tiles, in the list underneath | "**Not captured**" — for the exact six areas showing a coloured bar |
| The handover document | "**9 entries** across 1 of **10** areas" |
| The area page for pricing | "Kira holds **1 thing** about this part of your business" |

Nine areas everywhere in the app; ten in the document I'd hand a buyer. Seven things on
the screen; nine in the document. One thing on the pricing page; six unfiled items about
pricing on the Genome page.

Individually each of those is defensible — you're distinguishing *what I said in the
questionnaire* from *what she has actually captured*, and the copy does explain it if
you read all of it. I did read all of it. But I have run a business for thirty-five
years on the principle that if the stock count disagrees with the invoice, you stop and
find out why before you do anything else. Six different totals for the same pile is not
a wording problem, it's a counting problem, and it is the one thing that would make me
distrust the $210,000 as well.

**Opportunity:** one counter, computed once, printed everywhere. And if "you told us in
the questionnaire" is genuinely a different state, give it its own visual — a dotted
outline, not a filled bar. A filled bar is a promise.

## 4. The doors — fitted, then locked

This is the headline.

I clicked into "How work is priced and quoted". Good page. Plain question at the top:
*"A buyer's advisor asks: Could someone else reach his number?"* — that is precisely
the question, and it landed. And there was a button:

> **Tell Kira about how work is priced and quoted**

That is the door I said was missing. Well done.

I clicked it and **it put me back on the dashboard.** No message, no error, no
explanation. Just the same "add your business details" nag I had already dismissed
twice.

I checked all nine. Every one of the nine area pages points at the same place. So does
the "Talk to Kira" button on the big empty panel on the Genome page. **Ten buttons, all
of them the primary action on their screen, all of them landing me back where I
started.**

Here is the part that matters: it is not broken forever. It is broken **until you have a
Kira**, and a brand-new account does not have one. I only got past it by chance — the
dashboard has a different button, "Start talking to Kira →", which goes somewhere else
entirely and does work. After I'd been through that, the nine doors opened normally.

So every single person who signs up hits this in their first ten minutes, and the ones
who go to the Genome first — which is what the whole product is about, and where the
"Talk to Kira" button is — hit it before they ever find the one that works.

I would have closed the tab. I only didn't because you asked me to look properly.

And when the door does open, it opens on the wrong room. The link carries the area with
it, but the chat that comes up says "Ready when you are" and nothing else. It has no
idea I asked about pricing. If I press a button saying *tell her about pricing*, she
should open her mouth first, on pricing.

**Opportunity:** if there's no Kira yet, don't redirect — say so and make the button
build one. Ten dead buttons is one line of routing; the trust it costs is not
recoverable in one line.

## 5. The way in to talking to her, once you find it

The one that works is labelled "Start talking to Kira →" and the page says *"Tell her
how the work actually gets done... just talk to her the way you would to a person."*

I have no microphone, so I took the typed option — "No microphone, or prefer to type?
Write your brief instead", which is a proper fallback and I'm glad it's there.

It is not a conversation. It's a form: **Your name / Location / What do you want to work
on?** Then a second screen headed **"Review Your Framework"**, with fields called
**Primary Objective**, **Key Context**, **Success Definition**, **Constraints**, and a
button that says **Create My Kira**.

Four things wrong with that, in order of how much they'd stop me:

1. **"Create My Kira."** I already have one. I paid to have one. The FAQ on your website
   says I can have more than one, one per business. So am I about to create a second? Am
   I about to overwrite the first? Nothing on the screen says. That is a
   consequence-clarity failure on the only irreversible-looking button in the app, and I
   sat looking at it for a while before pressing it.
2. **The language.** Primary Objective, Key Context, Constraints, Framework. That is
   consultant-speak, and you have spent your whole website carefully not talking like
   that. It reads as if a different product got bolted on here — and it looks like one
   too: black and orange panel sitting inside a white and purple app, with your sidebar
   cut off halfway down the screen.
3. **The status line underneath said "Listening for your framework..."** while nothing
   was listening.
4. **My own words got clipped.** The Primary Objective box showed my paragraph with the
   last line cut off mid-sentence — and the line it cut was "Nobody at work knows that
   yet." Of all the sentences to truncate.

Then "Creating your Kira..." for a good half a minute with nothing else on screen.

**Opportunity:** an existing customer clicking "talk to her" should land in the chat.
Full stop. Keep the framework builder for signup, and rename every field on it into
English.

## 6. Talking to her — and yes, it does change the Genome

I told her how I price: an hourly rate I haven't moved in three years, materials at cost
plus twenty, then I look at the job and add or take off depending on the client and how
busy we are, and nobody else here can do it.

Her reply was a clean, accurate summary and a sensible offer. Good.

Then it went sideways.

> **Me:** Yes please. And put it in the Genome under how work is priced and quoted.
> **Her:** I've drafted a clear pricing and quoting guide... **I don't have an email
> address for you to send it from. What email should I use?**

My email address is printed in the corner of that same screen. And I never said anything
about sending it anywhere.

> **Me:** I do not want it emailed to anybody. I asked you to save it into my Genome.
> **Her:** ...saving a guide directly into the Genome as a fact or note isn't something I
> do **through dispatch**.

*Dispatch.* I'm sixty-six and I run electricians. I know what dispatch means on a job
board. I have no idea what it means here, and I shouldn't have to.

She then held onto the "guide" as something "still waiting for your go-ahead **before
sending**" — three more times — until I made her read it out. When she did, it was four
sentences of my own words rearranged into the third person. No rate, no worked example,
no rule for when I add and when I take off, nothing another human could actually quote
a job from. For $999 a month I could have written that in the van.

**But — and this is the good news — it did move the Genome.** The pricing tile went
from orange "You told us" to green "Just started", the whole page grew a proper document
underneath it, and the entry appeared. That was the question you asked me and the answer
is yes, plainly, it works.

**Opportunity:** she needs to stop treating everything as an outbound email. And when
she says she's drafted a guide, there should be a guide somewhere I can open — not a
sentence in a chat bubble claiming one exists.

## 7. The six things she was holding, and who this is working for

You asked me to read the unfiled pile and tell you how it made me feel.

Here are the six, verbatim:

> · The owner prefers not to have the pricing and quoting guide emailed but saved
>   directly in the Genome for reference.
> · A clear concise pricing and quoting method description is saved into the Genome under
>   how work is priced and quoted.
> · Only the owner currently performs quoting; no one else in the business is authorized
>   or trained to do so.
> · Quotes are adjusted up or down depending on the client and current workload.
> · Materials are charged at cost plus 20%.
> · The business prices jobs using an hourly rate that has not changed in three years.

The last four are my business. Correct, useful, exactly right.

**The first two are not about my business at all. They are about my argument with the
software.** One is a note that I told her off for trying to email something. The other is
a note that she filed something. Neither has anything to do with an electrical
contracting company, and both are sitting in a list headed *"written the way they will
appear in your handover document."*

So I opened the handover document. Nine entries. Here is what a buyer would read:

- The nine areas — **every single one** — say "Nothing recorded here yet. This is still
  carried by the owner alone." Including pricing, which is the one I'd just spent twenty
  minutes on and which the app says has an entry in it.
- Everything I said is dumped at the bottom under "Recorded, not yet filed", including:
  - *"All quotes are prepared solely by the owner; no one else in the business can
    prepare quotes."*
  - *"Currently, all quotes are prepared solely by the owner; no other staff member is
    authorized or able to produce quotes."*
  - *"Only the owner currently performs quoting; no one else in the business is
    authorized or trained to do so."*

  That is the same fact three times. The hourly rate appears twice.
- And this, in the document I would hand to a broker:
  > *"The owner prefers not to have pricing and quoting guides emailed or sent externally
  > and **insists** on retaining control of document distribution."*

How does it make me feel about who it's working for? Honestly — **it reads like it is
working for itself.** It has carefully recorded its own filing habits and my irritation
with it, characterised me to a stranger as someone who *insists* on things, and left
every part of my actual business marked "carried by the owner alone." If I'd pressed
Share without reading it — and Share is one click at the top of the page — that is what
would have gone out under my name.

**Two things save it, and they are big.** First: the private line. The bit where I said
I'm thinking of selling and nobody at work knows — I checked both documents. It is in my
own copy and it is **not** in the buyer's copy. That promise holds, and you should say so
louder, because it is the only reason a man in my position starts at all. Second: the
Share dialog itself is the best-written screen in the app — it tells me what it sends,
that private items stay out, that everyone can see who else got it, and offers "Read
what they will get" *before* the send button. That is exactly right.

**Opportunity:** two fixes and this section becomes the strongest part of the product.
De-duplicate before writing — three phrasings of one fact makes it look like a machine
wrote it, which is the one thing a buyer must not think. And never let a note about the
software's own behaviour reach a document about my business; if she must remember that I
don't like email, keep it where she keeps the valuation note ("Other things she has
noted"), not in the handover pile.

## 8. Smaller things, in the order I hit them

- **The money page came up blank once.** I clicked "Start building your Business Genome"
  off the valuation and got a white screen. I waited ten seconds, then longer. Nothing.
  A reload fixed it; I couldn't make it happen again on a second try. Once is enough on
  the page where you ask for a card.
- **The dashboard asked me to do the valuation I had just done.** It said "There's a
  business valuation saved on this device... That's mine — use it", and directly
  underneath, "0 of 3 done — start with where the business stands today. Thirteen short
  questions." When I clicked "That's mine", it said "Bringing the rest of the page up to
  date…" and then sat there for a good ten to fifteen seconds before the checklist
  changed. Long enough that I thought it had failed.
- **"See what has moved"** on the dashboard goes to the Genome, which shows the same
  baseline numbers and "0 of 9 well covered". Nothing has moved and nothing shows a
  delta. Don't name a link after something it doesn't do.
- **"Check this area"** — after my conversation, the pricing page grew a new button. It
  says "Kira holds 1 thing about this part of your business. Check this area." I pressed
  it and got: *"Nothing could be checked yet. Talk to Kira about this area first."* On
  the same card that says she holds one thing, with a "talk to her" button that
  redirected me to the dashboard. That's a closed loop.
- **The area page hasn't updated its summary.** It still tells me "How you decide what to
  charge is almost certainly yours alone — nothing you told us suggests otherwise", after
  I told her exactly how I decide what to charge.
- **Settings says I have no subscription.** "There's no card on your account, so nothing
  can be charged" and "You don't have a subscription yet, so there's nothing to manage."
  I came in on an invitation code, so that may be right — but nothing anywhere tells me
  what I'm actually on, and the page I signed up through quoted me $999 + GST a month.
  A man who thinks he has paid, reading that he has no subscription, gets on the phone.
- **"$0.00 of $20 included usage."** Twenty dollars of what? It's the only figure in the
  app with no explanation attached.
- **"authorized" and "authorized or trained"** — American spelling, twice, in an
  Australian document with an ABN on it.
- **The "Report a problem" tab follows me and sits on top of things.** On the Genome at
  full screen it covers the corner of the top-right area tile. On the phone it sits
  directly across the **Transferability 30/100** figure — one of your three headline
  numbers, obscured by a support button.
- **The chat page carries two lots of chrome.** Marketing header on top, app navigation
  underneath, and two footers. It looks like two websites glued together.

## 9. Would I keep paying $999 + GST a month?

Not yet. But I'd stay in touch, and last time I wouldn't have.

Here's my arithmetic, since you built a product that respects arithmetic. You told me
the gap is $210,000 and the fee is 5.7% a year of it. I accept that framing — it's fair,
and the fact that the band is set by my profit and not by the gap you calculated is the
single most trustworthy design decision in the whole thing.

What I'm buying, though, is the document. And I have now read the document. It says my
business has nothing written down, repeats three sentences about quoting, and describes
me to a buyer as a man who insists on things. I would not send that to a broker. So this
month I'd be paying $999 for something I can't use yet, on a promise that it gets better,
from a product where ten of its own buttons led back to the front door.

Fix three things and I'd sign tomorrow:

1. **The doors.** No new customer should ever click "tell her about this" and land on the
   dashboard.
2. **The document.** De-duplicate, file things into the nine areas properly, and keep
   notes about the software out of it. It is the product; everything else is packaging.
3. **One count.** Nine areas, one total, everywhere.

And keep the valuation exactly as it is. It's the best thing you've built and it's the
reason I gave you a second hour.

## Scope note — what I did not test

- **Voice.** No microphone on this machine. Everything above went through the typed
  fallback, which exists and works. The spoken experience is untested, and since that is
  the product's main interface, most of what I've said about "talking to her" is really
  about typing to her.
- **One conversation, one area.** I talked about pricing and nothing else. I don't know
  whether the other eight areas behave the same way once she has something in them.
- **I did not send anything.** I opened the Share dialog, read it, and closed it. I never
  put an address in, so I have not tested delivery, the reply-to, or what a recipient
  actually receives.
- **I did not press "Complete project" or "Share Kira"** — two options in the chat's
  "More" menu with no explanation attached to either. I don't know what they do, which
  is itself worth noting.
- **Google Drive, the raw data export, "Delete account", and the notifications toggles**
  are all untested.
- **Billing is untested end to end.** I came in on an invitation code, so no card was
  taken and no charge exists. I cannot tell you whether the arrears billing, the three
  days' notice or the one-click cancel actually work — only that they are described
  clearly.
- **One session, roughly fifty minutes, one browser, desktop then phone-width.** I did
  not test on a real phone.
- **The blank `/plan` page happened once and I could not reproduce it** on a second
  attempt through the same route or on three direct loads. I'm reporting what I saw, not
  a diagnosis.
