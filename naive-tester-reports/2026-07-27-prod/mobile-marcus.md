Hi Dennis,

**Platform Feedback — Kira Walkthrough**
**Persona:** Mobile Marcus — electrical/plumbing contracting business, ~15 staff, phone only, standing in a car park between jobs, one-handed, patchy signal
**URL:** https://kira-rho.vercel.app
**Goal:** Find out what my business is worth and whether I could sell it — on my phone, in about the time it takes to drink a servo coffee
**Viewport:** 375 x 667 (iPhone SE / mini)
**Duration:** ~40 minutes

---

## First impressions — the landing page

- The tab said **"Kira — your fractional exec"**. Fine. Not one of those "Create Next App" ones my nephew showed me.
- Page fits my screen. Nothing runs off the side, I never had to swipe sideways. Good.
- Headline is big and I got it straight away: *your business is your biggest asset, do you know what it's worth*. That's exactly the thing that keeps me up. And **"Free · no sign-up"** right under the button is why I kept going instead of closing it.
- **First thing that annoyed me:** a black "Report a problem" tab sits over the bottom of the hero and covers the word **"worth?"** in "Do you actually know what it's worth?" — the one word the whole page is about. I hadn't even read the first sentence and something was already sitting on it. (screenshot 01)
- The little chart with **$150k / $600k / $2.5M** is the best bit of the page and I **could not read the labels**. WALK AWAY / TODAY / CAPTURED are 10px. The dollar figures are 14px. I pinched in twice. On a page selling me "see the number", the number is the smallest thing on it.
- The hamburger opens a clean drawer with How it works / Pricing / Advisors / About. That works. But **the hamburger itself is only 30px wide** — I hit the edge of the orange button next to it twice before I got it. And "Sign in" is squashed into two lines at 14px right beside a big orange button, so it's a coin-toss which one my thumb lands on.
- Footer has the company name, ABN and address, and working Privacy and Terms links. As someone who's been burned by an app that turned out to be run out of nowhere, that mattered.

**Opportunity:** The three-number chart is your whole pitch. Make those labels 16px+ and let the numbers breathe on mobile — right now the strongest argument on the page is the least legible thing on it.

---

## The valuation — getting started

- Tapped "Find out in 3 minutes". Landed on a proper page with a plain-English explanation of the three numbers I'd get. No jargon, no "leverage your enterprise value". I liked it.
- There's a **currency picker set to AUD** at the top. Good — I've used American tools that quote me in dollars and I never know which dollars.
- **Two header bars stacked** on top of each other though (a "Kira" bar, then another "Kira / Currency" bar). That's about 140px of my 667px screen gone before any content. On a phone that's a fifth of the screen doing nothing.
- Tapped Start. It says "a few questions"… it's **11 questions**. That's not 3 minutes standing up. It's not terrible — most are one tap — but tell me 11 up front and I'll decide properly instead of feeling ambushed at question 7.

**Opportunity:** Put "11 quick questions, about 3 minutes" on the Start button. Honesty up front costs you nothing and stops the drop-off at question 5.

---

## The questions themselves — where it got frustrating

- **The big one: the two floating buttons sit on top of the Next and Back buttons.** "Ask Kira" (purple pill, bottom-right) lands directly on top of **Next**. "Report a problem" (black tab, bottom-left) lands on top of **Back**. I measured it by eye and then by thumb: Ask Kira occupies roughly x238–355 / y599–647, and the Next button sits at roughly x215–330 / y575–620. They overlap. **I hit "Ask Kira" instead of "Next" twice.** (screenshot 05)
  That is the single worst thing in this whole product on a phone. The two most important controls in an 11-step form are covered by two things I never asked for.
- **The intro block repeats above every single question.** "What is your business actually worth? / For most owners, their business is their biggest asset…" stays pinned above the card, so on every one of the 11 questions the actual question starts about 475px down a 667px screen. I scrolled 11 times to read 11 questions I'd already agreed to answer. (screenshot 04)
- **The "Ask Kira" pill also covers the help text** under the question. On the turnover question the explanation reads "Total sales – everything the business invoices or takes in over a year, before any costs come out" and the pill sits across the middle of that sentence. On the results page it sits across "$802,375 — runs & sells without you". Same widget, three different times it covered something I needed.
- The industry search is good. I typed "electr" and got "Electrical & Mechanical Contracting" with "Construction" beside it, and the result box is a decent size to hit. No complaints. (screenshot 10)
- The money fields brought up the **number keypad**, not the full keyboard. Whoever did that, thank you. Field's big too.
- But there are **no thousand separators while you type**. I put in my turnover and stared at "2800000" trying to count zeros with one thumb on a bright screen. Show it as **$2,800,000** as I type. Also the little up/down spinner arrows are a desktop thing — they're about 15px tall and useless on a phone.
- **Genuinely clever bit:** on the profit question it said *"That's a 17% margin on the $1,200,000 turnover you entered. Looks right?"* That's a real person's check. It caught me thinking. More of that.
- Back button works and keeps what you already put in. Tested it, went back two questions, my answers were still there.

**Opportunity:** Move Back/Next into a fixed bottom bar of their own and push the floating widgets above it (or hide them entirely inside the form). And collapse that intro block after question 1 — I've started, I don't need re-selling every screen.

---

## The result — this is the good part

- Three stacked cards, huge readable numbers: **Walk away $250,000 / Worth today $470,369 / With your knowledge captured $802,375**, then "the value locked inside your head right now: $332,006". I got it in three seconds without pinching. (screenshot 06)
- Then a section that actually explains *why*, in words I'd use: *a buyer isn't paying for last year's profit, they're paying for how confident they can be it keeps coming once you're gone.* The **used-car service-history comparison** landed instantly. That's the first time an app has explained a business valuation to me in a way I could repeat to my wife.
- Four line items showing where the value is hiding — owner dependence +$115,856, documented systems +$115,280, recurring revenue +$57,640, client spread +$43,230 — each with a plain sentence. That's a to-do list, not a report.
- A **transferability score of 36/100**. Blunt. I didn't like the number and that's the point.
- The disclaimer names its source: sector-median multiples from BizBuySell's 2025 data, ~9,500 closed deals, and says plainly it's not a formal valuation. I trust a number more when it tells me where it came from.
- "Save / print this" and "Start over" are both there. On a phone I'd want **"Text this to myself"** or an email — I'm not printing anything from a car park, and if I close this tab I'm fairly sure it's gone.

**Opportunity:** Add "email me this result" on the results screen. It's the one moment I'd hand over an email address willingly, and right now the only way to keep the number is a screenshot.

---

## Losing my signal / coming back later

- I couldn't get a clean test of a dropout because the browser kept getting pulled around, so take this with a caveat — but from what I could see, **your answers live in the page, not on a server**. Every time I loaded the valuation page fresh it dropped me back to the intro with no "pick up where you left off". The saved blob it does write only survives as long as the tab does.
- For me that's a real risk. I get interrupted constantly. If I'm at question 8, the phone rings, and Safari dumps the tab to save memory — which it does — I start again at question 1. I would not start again.

**Opportunity:** Persist the answers properly (even anonymously) and show "Resume — you were at question 8 of 11" when I come back. That single change probably saves you a chunk of half-finished valuations.

---

## Signing in — I couldn't

- The login page is the tidiest screen in the product. **Forgot password** link, an **eye icon** to show the password, **and** a magic-link option, all full-width and easy to hit one-handed. (screenshot 07)
- But **the account I was given doesn't work.** I typed it in carefully, twice, with real keystrokes: *"That email and password don't match. Try again, or use the magic-link or reset-password options below."* (screenshot 08)
- Credit where due — that's a good error message. It doesn't just say "invalid", it tells me my next two options. Most apps don't bother.
- Because of that, **I never got inside the product**. So I can't tell you anything about the nav once you're logged in, whether there's a settings page, or whether Sign Out is where it should be. Flagging that as untested, not as passed.
- One more: the black "Report a problem" tab covers the "Need an account? Sign up" link at the bottom of the login card. Third page in a row where that widget sits on something.

**Opportunity:** Whatever's wrong with that account, the bigger lesson is the error copy is already doing its job — the failure is upstream. Worth confirming the QA login actually works before the next round of testing, because right now nobody can check the logged-in half.

---

## The voice thing

- There's an "Ask Kira" button on the valuation page — one tap, no hunting. Tapped it and got a small panel: **"Need help? / Start a call"**. (screenshot 09)
- On my screen it's a little floating box in the corner, not a full-screen thing, and the "Powered by…" line at the bottom is **cut off by the edge of the screen**.
- Honestly? Standing in a car park with a truck idling next to me, I'm not starting a voice call. But I'd want to know it's there for when I'm sitting in the ute. It being one tap is right.

**Opportunity:** Make the voice panel a proper full-screen sheet on a phone, and stop it landing on the Next button.

---

## Would I carry on?

Yes — but only just, and only because the result at the end was worth the fight.

The number and the explanation are genuinely good. Better than the accountant's version, because it told me *what to do about it*. If someone had shown me that results screen first I'd have done the 11 questions happily.

What nearly lost me was the middle: hitting "Ask Kira" when I meant "Next", scrolling past the same paragraph eleven times, and squinting at a chart that's meant to be the hook. None of that is hard to fix. All of it is the difference between me finishing and me putting the phone back in my pocket.
