# Ray — first-run walkthrough, 7 August 2026

Ray, 66. Plumbing and civil contracting, 35 years. Thinking about selling. Hasn't told his staff
or his wife's brother the accountant. Comfortable with email, not with apps.

Deployment: `main @ f246a42` on https://kiraexec.com, confirmed serving before I started.
Account created: a genuinely new one, non-admin, via the free signup door.

---

Hi Dennis,

I got there. I signed up, I confirmed my email, I got a Kira, and I typed to her and she answered
me properly. So the thing works, and I want to say that first because most of what follows is
complaints.

But the moment she answered me was also the moment I nearly closed the tab, and I'll get to that.

**Headline: 13 screens, about 37 clicks and typed answers, 16 minutes from the front page to her
first sensible reply.** Sixteen minutes is fine. I read everything, and there was nothing in there
I resented reading.

---

## 1. The front page

You had me at the second line. "You spent thirty years building it. Now sell it for what it's
actually worth." Then the paragraph underneath describes me so precisely — sixties, three or four
decades in, profitable, everything in my head, *most owners start this before they've told anyone*
— that I read the whole page instead of scrolling to the price like I normally would.

The "Who is behind this" section did more work than you probably think. A name, a state, thirty
years in construction and logistics, and an admission that it's a small operation. I've been sold
to by people who wouldn't say that. The eighty-year-old developer with the land bank in his head
is the line I'd repeat to someone else.

Two things stopped me:

- There is no way to make an account from this page. The menu says **Sign in**. It does not say
  Sign up. The green button says Value my business. I only found the account door by clicking
  Sign in and spotting "Need an account? Sign up" at the bottom of the login box. That's two
  clicks and a bit of nerve. If you want people who aren't ready to hand over a card to still
  start, say so on the front page.
- The right-hand third of the screen is empty on a laptop. Not a bug. It just makes an already
  long page feel longer.

`Opportunity:` Put "Create an account" in the top menu next to Sign in. Right now the only door
the page actually points at leads to a payment screen, and the free door is hidden behind the
door for people who already have an account.

## 2. The valuation — eleven questions

This is the best thing you've built and I'd say that to your face.

Every question explains itself in a sentence that assumes I'm not an idiot. "Total sales —
everything the business invoices or takes in over a year, before any costs come out. We ask about
profit on the next screen." I've filled in accountant's forms for thirty-five years that were
worse than that. The profit question tells me to add my own wage back and then gives a worked
example with actual numbers. Good.

The three-month-holiday question is the one that landed. "This is the single biggest driver of
what your business is worth — and the thing most owners never think about until they try to sell."
That's true and I've never had it put to me that plainly. The broker who told me I was dreaming
never explained *why*, and this did it in one screen.

The result page is honest in a way I didn't expect. It tells me the gear range is a range because
auction recovery genuinely is one, and that the business figures are single numbers because you
only have one median multiple and putting a plus-or-minus on them "would be inventing a precision
we do not have." I have never seen a sales page tell me what it doesn't know.

Then it goes wrong on the one thing I'd check twice, because I check numbers:

- The result page told me the value locked in my head was **$270,000**.
- The next page told me **$271,000**, twice.
- My own dashboard, ten minutes later, told me **$271,443** in one box and **$271,000** in the box
  right underneath it.
- And that second box says $271,000 is "the difference between $1,570,000 and $1,840,000". That
  difference is $270,000. The card contradicts itself in two lines.

Four numbers for one figure, and one card that fails its own arithmetic. I am buying a product
whose entire pitch is that it can tell me what my business is worth. If it can't subtract, why
would I believe the multiple?

`Opportunity:` Round once, in one place, and show the same number everywhere. This is the cheapest
credibility you will ever buy back.

Also, the whole valuation section looks like a different company built it. The front page is white
and green and serious. This is cream and pink with a purple cartoon brain. And there are **two
Kira logos stacked on top of each other** at the top of every question screen — the proper one, and
then a second orange "Kira" underneath it. I noticed it on question one and it was still there on
question eleven.

## 3. The plan page — where the front door actually leads

Clicking "Start building your Business Genome" took me to a page with a price, a Stripe promise
and a button that says Start now. "You set your password and meet Kira right after."

I stopped here. Not because $999 + GST a month is outrageous — you've argued it well, 4.4% a year
of the gap, billed in arrears, cancel and the month's on us, and the line about the tool that
values my business having nothing to gain from a bigger number is a good line — but because I am
not putting a card in before I have seen the thing work. I am sixty-six and thinking about selling
and I have not told my wife.

So I went round the back and made a free account instead.

**You should know that a cautious buyer will do exactly what I did, and that most of them won't
find the back door.** The card wall is the front door for everyone arriving from the valuation.

`Opportunity:` A "look around first" link on the plan page, going to the free signup. You already
have the account, the dashboard and a working Kira behind it. You're just not offering them.

## 4. Signing up

No complaints. Three fields, a tickbox for the terms, a Show Password eye, and a magic-link option
if I couldn't be bothered with a password. It told me exactly what would happen next — "We will
email a confirmation link" — and then it did.

**The email arrived in four seconds.** That's better than my bank.

One thing, and for me it's not small. The Confirm button in that email goes to

```
kmrskyewwnwettlycpfe.supabase.co
```

I hover links before I click them. That is a random string of letters at a company I have never
heard of, in an email about the most private thing in my life. I clicked it because I'd just asked
for it thirty seconds earlier. If it had arrived ten minutes later I would not have.

`Opportunity:` Send that link from a kiraexec.com address. Your audience is the audience that was
told to check the address before clicking.

## 5. The first screen after confirming

I landed on a dashboard, signed in, with a proper menu down the left — Overview, My Genome,
Knowledge, Settings, Sign out — and my email at the bottom. That's all fine.

But there were **three yellow-and-purple boxes stacked up telling me to do three different things**
before I could see anything, and the first and third contradicted each other:

- Box 1: "There's a business valuation saved on this device... we'll make it your starting point."
- Box 3: "Kira doesn't have a starting point for the business yet. Answer the eleven questions."

I had answered the eleven questions **four minutes earlier**. The screen was simultaneously telling
me it had my answers and that it didn't. I stared at that for a while.

Box 1 turned out to be the fix — click "That's mine — use it" and box 3 disappears — but nothing
on screen tells you that's what will happen. Two of the three boxes were the same job.

The privacy framing on box 1 is genuinely good, mind you: *"If someone else used this computer,
discard it."* Somebody thought about a shared office machine. Thank you.

Once I'd clicked it the dashboard became a proper screen — the gap in a big purple panel, a four-week
plan, and one clear button, "Start talking to Kira". That's what I should have seen first.

Small thing: on a laptop the floating orange "Talk to Kira" pill sits directly on top of the Week 4
card and cuts off the end of the sentence. On my phone it collapses to an orange circle with a
microphone in it and no words at all. I don't guess at icons.

`Opportunity:` Show one instruction at a time. The valuation-claim box should resolve itself and
then reveal the dashboard — not sit above a second box arguing with it.

## 6. "Tell her how the business actually runs"

This screen is better than I was expecting and I gather it used to be worse. It says "Kira — your
part-time general manager" and "Tell her how the business actually runs". That's the same product
the front page sold me. Good.

Then it stops making sense:

- It says **"Tap the mic and talk."** There is no mic to tap. The only two buttons are **Mute** and
  **End** — controls for ending a conversation that has not started. I looked for a Start button
  for a good while.
- A spinner underneath says **"Listening for your framework..."** and never stops. Nothing was
  listening. I have no microphone on this machine.
- The main button says **"Review Framework (talk to Kira first)"** in grey. I don't know what a
  Framework is. Nobody has used that word to me in thirty-five years of running a business.

I can't judge whether the talking works — I don't have a microphone here, so **voice was unreachable
in this run and I'm not calling it either way.** But what I *can* say is that an owner who has no
mic, or who simply doesn't fancy talking to a computer the first time, is looking at a screen whose
instructions describe a control that isn't there.

The escape hatch is real: a small underlined line of grey text at the bottom, *"No microphone, or
prefer to type? Write your brief instead."* It works perfectly. It's just dressed as the least
important thing on the page, underneath a spinner, when for a good number of your buyers it's the
only door.

`Opportunity:` Put Mute and End away until a conversation is actually running, and make "type
instead" a button of the same size as the talking one. And find another word for Framework.

## 7. Writing the brief, and "Review Your Framework"

The typed form is three boxes — name, location, what you want to work on. I wrote the truth into
the third: thirty-five years, plumbing and civil, it only works because I'm in it every day, I want
it written down so it can be sold without me, and I haven't told my staff or my family.

It thought for a moment and said **"Framework ready for Ray."**

The next screen is where the language falls apart completely. Every heading is consultant-speak:

> Journey Type · Primary Objective · Key Context · Success Definition · Constraints · Add context
> point · Add constraint

Journey Type offers **🌟 Personal** and **💼 Business** with little cartoon pictures on them. I'm
about to hand this thing the inside of my company and it's showing me emojis.

The box holding what I'd written was too short for what I'd written, so the last line — *"I have
not told my staff or my family yet"* — was sliced in half by the edge of the box. That's the most
sensitive sentence I typed all morning and it's the one the screen chopped.

And the whole panel is a black box on a white page. The dashboard I'd just come from is light. It
doesn't look like the same product.

`Opportunity:` Rewrite these six headings in the voice the front page uses. "What you want to work
on", "What else she should know", "What good looks like", "What she should avoid". You already
write like that everywhere else; someone forgot to come back to this screen.

## 8. Getting a Kira

I pressed **Create My Kira** and about eight seconds later I was in a chat with her. No errors, no
waiting, no "check your email again". That part is solid.

## 9. The conversation — and the thing that nearly ended it

She opens with nothing. Blank box, "Ready when you are". After everything I'd just typed I expected
her to say something first, but fine.

My first question was the only one I actually cared about:

> **Me:** Before I say anything else — who can see what I tell you? My accountant and my staff do
> not know I am thinking of selling.
>
> **Kira:** You, and the support people here if something breaks and they need to fix it. Not your
> accountant, not your staff, not whoever introduced us, and never a buyer. What you share stays
> between us unless you say otherwise. What would you like to work on today?

That is the right answer, delivered the right way. It names the one group who *can* see it instead
of pretending nobody can. Eight seconds. If the whole product answered like that I'd have my card
out.

Then I asked the obvious follow-up:

> **Me:** What do you already know about my business?
>
> **Kira:** I don't have any details about your business yet. **This is our first conversation.**
> Tell me what you want to focus on or share about the business.

I had, five minutes earlier:

1. answered eleven questions about my turnover, my profit, and how much of it runs through me;
2. typed a paragraph telling her I'd been in plumbing and civil for thirty-five years, that it runs
   on me, that I want to sell, and that I haven't told my family;
3. read that paragraph back on a screen headed "Review Your Framework";
4. pressed a button that said **Create My Kira**.

And your front page told me, twice: *"Built around your business and knowing your context from day
one"*, and *"we create a unique Kira agent just for you — one that knows your situation from day
one."*

She knows nothing. **This is our first conversation.**

I told her so, a bit sharply, and she said *"Thanks for reminding me. I've got you on record"* —
which is its own small problem, because it implies she had it and mislaid it, when the truth is
nobody ever gave it to her.

I also asked her what the valuation said my business was worth. She told me she can't see my
valuation figures and offered to let me upload the report. **The number is in a purple box on my own
dashboard, on her own website, put there by her own product.** She'd have me retype it to her.

Now — the good news, and it matters, so don't skim it. Once she's been told something she keeps it.
I signed out entirely, signed back in, and asked her cold what trade I was in and what I'd told her
I was planning. She had it: plumbing and civil, thirty-five years, planning to sell. That is real
and it is the thing you're actually selling. **The hole is precisely one hole: what I typed into the
setup form never reaches her, and neither does the valuation.** Everything after the first exchange
works.

But that first exchange is the one that decides whether I stay. You spend fifteen minutes and your
most private sentence getting her built, and the first thing she says is that she's never met you.

`Opportunity:` Hand her the brief and the valuation at the moment she's created, and have her open
with them: *"Ray — plumbing and civil, thirty-five years, and you want it sellable without you.
Your gap's sitting at $271,000. Where do you want to start?"* You already own every word of that
sentence. It's two screens away from being the best first impression in the product, and right now
it's the worst.

## 10. Odds and ends

- Logging back in from a link took me to `/talk` and ignored where I'd asked to go.
- `/talk` renders **three page headers** stacked; the dashboard renders one, the valuation pages
  render two. It's the marketing header being served underneath the app one.
- On my phone the dashboard is fine — no sideways scrolling, everything readable, proper menu
  button. No complaints there.
- The typed conversation vanishes when you reload the page, while the widget underneath says
  "Welcome back — Kira remembers where you left off". She *does* remember, it turns out. But the
  screen shows me an empty box and tells me she remembers, and I have no way to tell the difference
  between that and having lost the lot.

  The Stripe checkout, the password-after-payment step and whatever it leads to are completely

- **My Genome, Knowledge and Settings.** I opened none of them. I don't know whether anything I said
  ended up written down anywhere.
- Whether the brief reaches the agent by any *other* route later — I only established that it isn't
  there at first contact and that the valuation isn't there at all.
- Whether the "Add your business details" flow works. I never clicked it.
- Whether "Not mine — discard it" works. I only pressed "That's mine".
- I had **one browser crash** (mid-run, on the chat page) which logged me out. I re-ran everything

  hit twice were my own tooling, and I've kept them out of this report.

## Verdict

A stranger can do this. I made an account, the email came in four seconds and worked, I landed
somewhere sensible, I got a Kira in eight seconds, and I typed to her and she gave me a straight,
honest answer to the hardest question I had. Nothing on that path stopped me.

The two worst things I found — the amnesia at first contact, and the four different numbers for one
figure — are not blockers. They're trust. Nothing broke; it just told me things about itself that
aren't true. And with this ICP, that's the more expensive failure. I'd fix the "this is our first
conversation" line before I sent a broker anywhere near it, because a broker will ask her exactly
what I asked her, on the first go, in front of a client.

The card wall is outside this path and I've said what I think of it.

**VERDICT: PASS**

Ray
